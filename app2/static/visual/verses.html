Ephesians 2:8–9

Tap a part of the diagram to light its words in the Greek and the Berean.

The Greek · each word opens its card
The Berean Standard Bible

For it is by grace you have been saved through faith, and this not from yourselves; it is the gift of God, not by works, so that no one can boast.

What the verse says, as it is built

Drawn from the Greek's own grammar: γάρ (for), διά (through), καὶ τοῦτο οὐκ (and this not), οὐκ ἐξ (not from), ἵνα (so that). σεσῳσμένοι is a perfect: saved, and it stands. “This” (τοῦτο) is neuter, while grace and faith are feminine, so readers differ on whether it points to faith, to grace, or to the whole of being saved. The diagram shows how the sentence is built and takes no side.

Who and what it names

The Berean Standard Bible

Do not be conformed to this world, but be transformed by the renewing of your mind. Then you will be able to test and approve what is the good, pleasing, and perfect will of God.

What the verse says, as it is built

Drawn from the Greek's own grammar: μή … ἀλλά (not … but) sets the two ways against each other; τῇ ἀνακαινώσει (by the renewing) gives the means; εἰς τό with the infinitive gives what follows, to test and approve. αἰών is “age” (the Berean: “world”). μεταμορφοῦσθε is the verb used of the transfiguration (Matthew 17:2). John 15:5

Tap a part of the diagram to light its words in the Greek and the Berean.

The Greek · each word opens its card
The Berean Standard Bible

I am the vine and you are the branches. The one who remains in Me, and I in him, will bear much fruit. For apart from Me you can do nothing.

What the verse says, as it is built

Drawn from the verse's own grammar. Two statements of who is who: I am the vine, you are the branches. Then ὁ μένων … οὗτος: the one who remains is the one who bears much fruit, and remaining runs both ways, “in Me, and I in him.” ὅτι (for) gives the reason: χωρὶς ἐμοῦ, apart from Me, nothing. The diagram adds nothing the verse does not say.

Isaiah 52:7

Tap a part of the diagram to light its words in the Hebrew and the Berean.

The Hebrew · each word opens its card
The Berean Standard Bible

How beautiful on the mountains are the feet of those who bring good news, who proclaim peace, who bring good tidings, who proclaim salvation, who say to Zion, “Your God reigns!”

What the verse says, as it is built

Drawn as one messenger and the set of things said of him. In the Hebrew all five are singular participles of the same one: brings good news (מְבַשֵּׂר), proclaims (מַשְׁמִיעַ), says (אֹמֵר); the Berean reads “those who.” The fifth carries the message itself, “Your God reigns” (מָלַךְ אֱלֹהָיִךְ). Paul quotes the verse in Romans 10:15.

Who and what it names
