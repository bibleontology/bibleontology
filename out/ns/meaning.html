Bible Ontology — meaning

Namespace https://bibleontology.org/ns/meaning# · by Doug Egge · CC BY 4.0 · version 1.2.1 (this release, kept) · view: formatted · Turtle text · shapes

What a node is about: word, sense, referent, figure and reading, as five layers that share ids with the structure (https://bibleontology.org/ns/structure) and never merge with it. Senses and categories, figures and mentions are built against it; the joins between the two graphs are not yet built. The word layer's terms (bow:) are defined here and also answer at https://bibleontology.org/ns/word.

Reasoning policy

These terms describe the graph; they are not built for automated inference over it. Every relation in the graph is a claim that carries its verse, its certainty and, where someone in the text says it, its speaker, and a reasoner that ignores those turns a character's claim into the narrator's or a reading into a fact. So: no owl:sameAs between records (possibly_same_as and spoken_of_as are readings and names, never identities); no functional or inverse-functional kinship (the accounts name more than one father or mother for some people, and the graph keeps both); no transitive chaining of images, figures, symbols or places; and nothing about the records of God, the Father, Jesus or the Holy Spirit is derived by inference: they change only by a recorded editorial decision, as the accounts give them. The policy forbids derivations, not descriptions. Constraints are checked, closed-world, by the SHACL shapes at https://bibleontology.org/ns/shapes, not inferred.

Versioning

A term's address never changes and is never reused. A term whose meaning changes gets a new address, and the old one is marked owl:deprecated with dcterms:isReplacedBy. Additions raise the minor version; a change of meaning raises the major. Each release is kept forever at its dated version IRI; the undated address serves the latest.

Categories

categories

Top level: WordNet's lexicographer files (26 noun, 15 verb), free, standard, and complete by construction: every WordNet sense sits in exactly one. Below it, WordNet synsets reached through English glosses, as an overlay. Proper names are typed in layer 3, not here.

contested (readers divide)

Contested

Readers divide on whether the use is literal or figurative, and the text does not settle it: 'This is My body' (Mat 26:26), the bread is his body, or a sign of it; Ezekiel's temple (Ezk 40–48), a temple to be built, or God's presence among his people. The graph decides nothing: each side is a UsageReading credited to its reader, and a view can take the graph as either reads it, both kept and neither resolved. Not the same as Unmarked (no one has claimed anything), and not used where the text itself settles it: 'He was speaking about the temple of His body' (Jhn 2:21) is a stated figure.

sign (literal and figurative at once)

Sign

A real thing in the scene that also stands for something, where the text says so: the bow in the cloud is a bow and 'the sign of the covenant' (Gen 9:13); the stones from the Jordan are stones and 'a sign among you' (Jos 4:6). Repetition, extra attention, contrast or cultural meaning do not make a use a sign: Exodus repeats the tabernacle's boards for chapters, and they are boards. A Sign carries a FigurativeUse with tenorBasis 'stated'.
