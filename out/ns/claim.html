Bible Ontology — claims

Namespace https://bibleontology.org/ns/claim# · by Doug Egge · CC BY 4.0 · version 1.2.1 (this release, kept) · view: formatted · Turtle text · shapes

How the graph's relations are published: each as a claim that carries the verses it rests on, its certainty, and whose claim it is. The narrator's statements and a character's claims are never mixed: in the RDF 1.1 export a claim's plain triple is stated only inside the named graph of its voice; in the RDF 1.2 export only the narrator's triples are stated, and every other claim is described by the triple term it reifies, never stated. The narrator's claims are only what the text states or entails (claim:explicit, claim:implied); a conjectured or disputed claim, and the graph's own groupings (themes, patterns, story links, the text's divisions, what a passage speaks of), are credited to the editors' voice and described, never stated. Relation types are at https://bibleontology.org/ns/relation; records are the site's own pages, https://bibleontology.org/e/<id>, and verses https://bibleontology.org/p/<Book>.<chapter>.<verse>.

Reasoning policy

These terms describe the graph; they are not built for automated inference over it. Every relation in the graph is a claim that carries its verse, its certainty and, where someone in the text says it, its speaker, and a reasoner that ignores those turns a character's claim into the narrator's or a reading into a fact. So: no owl:sameAs between records (possibly_same_as and spoken_of_as are readings and names, never identities); no functional or inverse-functional kinship (the accounts name more than one father or mother for some people, and the graph keeps both); no transitive chaining of images, figures, symbols or places; and nothing about the records of God, the Father, Jesus or the Holy Spirit is derived by inference: they change only by a recorded editorial decision, as the accounts give them. The policy forbids derivations, not descriptions. Constraints are checked, closed-world, by the SHACL shapes at https://bibleontology.org/ns/shapes, not inferred.

Versioning

A term's address never changes and is never reused. A term whose meaning changes gets a new address, and the old one is marked owl:deprecated with dcterms:isReplacedBy. Additions raise the minor version; a change of meaning raises the major. Each release is kept forever at its dated version IRI; the undated address serves the latest.

claim

Claim

One relation the graph records: what it relates, the verses it rests on, how certain it is, and whose claim it is. The claim is published in every case; its plain triple is stated only in the graph of its voice (RDF 1.1 export) or only if its voice is the narrator's (RDF 1.2 export), and in either file only where it is a statement made outright (claim:modality) and stated or entailed (claim:certainty): a promise, command, question or negated clause, and a conjectured or disputed claim, is described in both and stated in neither.

reported by

reportedBy

For a voice whose words another speaker gives (speech within speech): the speaker who reports them. A prophet's 'This is what the LORD says', or 'Yet you say ...' (Job 22:13). The words are neither speaker's alone, so their claim is in this voice, not in either speaker's (in the RDF 1.1 export, their triple is stated in this voice's graph).

says

Used only as the relation of a reporting speaker's claim: that a person in the text says another claim. Its object is the reported CLAIM, which itself reifies its own triple, so each speaker's part is described and neither is stated (Jeremiah 32:7: Jeremiah's claim is that the LORD says the inner claim). The same in both export files.

the narrator

narrator

The voice of the text itself: what it states or entails, outside any character's speech; never a conjecture, and never the graph's own groupings, which are the editors' (https://bibleontology.org/voice/reader/editors). Its triples are the only ones stated in the RDF 1.2 export (the default graph); in the RDF 1.1 export, load only this graph to get only what the text states.
