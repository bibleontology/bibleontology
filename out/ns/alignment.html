Where a term here has a near equivalent in a standard vocabulary, this says which, and how near, so a tool that knows the standard can read our data. The mappings are SKOS mapping properties: a close match is near enough to use one for the other in most queries, never the same term; theirs is broader and theirs is narrower say which way the difference runs; related says the two belong together but are not interchangeable, and the note says how. There is no exact match: none of these terms was made for the same corpus. A mapping commits a reasoner to nothing beyond typing both ends as a SKOS concept, which SKOS itself entails; OWL tools read the mappings as annotations. A mapping says how the text presents a person, place or event; none takes a view on historicity. Every target was checked against its specification.

Not mapped, and why

OursWhy there is no mapping
bom:DeityNone of the five vocabularies mapped here has a class for gods. Deity reaches foaf:Agent through PersonalBeing; CIDOC's Actor is people, so it does not.
bom:HeavenlyBeingNone either; the same path through PersonalBeing to foaf:Agent.
bos:WordOne word at one place in one verse. OntoLex's Word is the dictionary entry (ours is bow:Lexeme, a subclass of ontolex:LexicalEntry), not a word in a text.
bos:ClauseNo standard vocabulary above has a clause; it comes from MACULA's syntax trees.
claim:voiceWhose claim a claim is. The standard pattern is attribution, which claim:claimedBy, claim:reader and claim:voiceOf already state as prov:wasAttributedTo.
bor:possibly_same_asA reader's view that two records may be one person. Not owl:sameAs and not schema:sameAs, which assert an identity; the reasoning policy forbids one.
