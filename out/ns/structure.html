Bible Ontology — structure

Namespace https://bibleontology.org/ns/structure# · by Doug Egge · CC BY 4.0 · version 1.2.1 (this release, kept) · view: formatted · Turtle text · shapes

How the text is built: clauses, what they assert and with what force, and how they group into turns, scenes and larger units. Shares ids with the meaning layer (https://bibleontology.org/ns/meaning) and never merges with it; the relations between records are at https://bibleontology.org/ns/relation. Where a relation joins one of several kinds, its domain or range here is the kind they all are, and the shapes at https://bibleontology.org/ns/shapes list which ones.

Reasoning policy

These terms describe the graph; they are not built for automated inference over it. Every relation in the graph is a claim that carries its verse, its certainty and, where someone in the text says it, its speaker, and a reasoner that ignores those turns a character's claim into the narrator's or a reading into a fact. So: no owl:sameAs between records (possibly_same_as and spoken_of_as are readings and names, never identities); no functional or inverse-functional kinship (the accounts name more than one father or mother for some people, and the graph keeps both); no transitive chaining of images, figures, symbols or places; and nothing about the records of God, the Father, Jesus or the Holy Spirit is derived by inference: they change only by a recorded editorial decision, as the accounts give them. The policy forbids derivations, not descriptions. Constraints are checked, closed-world, by the SHACL shapes at https://bibleontology.org/ns/shapes, not inferred.

Versioning

A term's address never changes and is never reused. A term whose meaning changes gets a new address, and the old one is marked owl:deprecated with dcterms:isReplacedBy. Additions raise the minor version; a change of meaning raises the major. Each release is kept forever at its dated version IRI; the undated address serves the latest.

Acts of one agent

AgentActs

Clauses in a row that are the acts of one agent: joined by no word, none a narrative 'and then' (wayyiqtol), each stating its subject only in its verb, and every one of those subjects resolved to the SAME referent. Author

Concession: the obstacle, what holds anyway, and why

Concession

Where the text marks a concession (גַּם כִּי, 'even though'): the conceded clause (the obstacle), the claim that holds anyway, and — where a כִּי gives it — that claim's ground. Where the held claim is NEGATED, the response the obstacle would have brought is named by the text and denied, and the group carries it as `denied`: Psa 23:4 'even though I walk through the valley of deep darkness, I will NOT FEAR harm, for you are with me' → conceded, holds anyway, its ground; denied 'fear'. That this is an overridden motivation is the reading over the group (meaning graph). The mirror of `failed_result`: there an act's aim does not come; here a circumstance's expected outcome does not, and the text says why.

Speech turn

Turn

A communication by speech, however many verses it runs. Open at the end of a verse that holds a speech and does not end on a narrative wayyiqtol outside it; continued by each following verse that does not open with a narrative wayyiqtol. TURNS NEST: the outermost is opened by a narrated saying that sits inside no speech; each saying inside its speech opens the next turn in, which is a `part` of the one around it. Each turn has a mode (performed / commanded) and, where the text gives them, a sender and an addressee. Continuing verses are parts of the innermost turn.

but / however

contrast

The text says 'but': אוּלָם or אֶפֶס כִּי before a clause — words that are a clause connective and nothing else. 'but let us fight them in the plain' (1Ki 20:23), 'however, the city was formerly named Luz' (Gen 28:19). Whether a contrast is also a concession is a reading. Mid-verse only, as every backward link: a verse-opening 'but' has no host in its verse.
