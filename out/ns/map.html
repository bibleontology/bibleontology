The map › 4 · Who says it, and how sure

A claim is stated as fact only when it is made outright; anything else is described and never stated. Below the claim, the scales and lists it draws from.

Built on open standards

RDF 1.2, and 1.1 beside it the standard ›

Built on open standards › RDF 1.2, and 1.1 beside it

Every claim reifies its statement as a triple term, so the statement can be described without being asserted. Only what the text itself states is also asserted; a speaker's claim, a reader's reading, a promise or a command stays described. 21,378 claims. RDF 1.2 is a W3C Candidate Recommendation, so the same claims are also published in RDF 1.1, for stores without triple terms: there each voice is a named graph, and a speaker's claim stays apart from the narrator's only if the store keeps its default graph apart from the named ones (Apache Jena does; RDF4J joins them). Everything else (the vocabularies, the checks, the typed records) is RDF 1.1 Turtle.

PROV-O the standard ›

Built on open standards › PROV-O

Who says a claim is a PROV attribution: claim:claimedBy, claim:reader and claim:voiceOf are kinds of prov:wasAttributedTo, and the export also states prov:wasAttributedTo outright, for tools without a reasoner. A claim a run produced names the run, and the run names its models. 13,266 claims attributed; 757 tied to a run.

SHACL the standard ›

Built on open standards › SHACL

Where RDFS cannot say "either", a published shape can: the subject of "told" must be one of 4 kinds. 177 shapes are published at /ns/shapes; 154 of them run against the published data, each test planting a fault that must be caught, and the 23 structure shapes wait for the structure export.

SKOS the standard ›

Built on open standards › SKOS

The values a claim may take are controlled lists, each a concept scheme with a definition per value: how sure a claim is, the force it is said with, the broad kind of act its verb names.

DQV the standard ›

Built on open standards › DQV

What a card shows first is decided by a published rule, not hidden in code: every card word carries its measured share and the tier it is shown in, and the policy names the cut. A published query rebuilds the shown view exactly, and a published shape checks that no word shown by default falls under the cut; a planted weak word must fail it. 5,804 measurements.

OWL 2 the standard ›

Built on open standards › OWL 2

Every term is typed so OWL tools read it rightly. Nothing is derived by a reasoner: where a link may join several kinds, the list is a SHACL shape, because in RDFS two domains mean both, not either.

Maps to the alignment file: /ns/alignment ›

Built on open standards › Maps to

Where a term here has a near equivalent in a standard vocabulary, the alignment file says which and how near, as SKOS mappings: 49 mappings, every target checked against its specification, and no exact matches. A mapping commits a reasoner to nothing beyond typing both ends as skos:Concept, which SKOS itself entails.

The sources it is read from

Built on open standards › The sources

MACULA Hebrew › Greek ›

Every word, its grammar and its sentence structure come from the MACULA Hebrew and Greek Linguistic Datasets, © Biblica, Inc., CC BY 4.0, and the original they give decides wherever a translation differs. Their texts are the Westminster Leningrad Codex and the Nestle 1904 Greek New Testament, with the J. Alan Groves Center's Hebrew syntax and the Open Scriptures Hebrew Bible morphology. The structure layer is read from them.
