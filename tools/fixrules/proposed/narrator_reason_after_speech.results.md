# narrator_reason_after_speech: run over the whole corpus (reviewed by Claude)

DETECT: 5 fired, and 2Sa.17.14 (the verse it was written for) was not among them. A sample of 3, all WRONG:
- 1Ki.13.18 | WRONG | "an angel spoke to me by the word of the LORD" is INSIDE the prophet's quoted speech (a quotation within a speech), not the narrator.
- Job.28.14 | WRONG | "the sea says, 'It is not with me'" is a second SPEECH, not a reason.
- Jdg.20.32 | WRONG | "But the Israelites said, 'Let us retreat'" is a new speech, not a reason.

What separates them: in all three the clause's own verb is a verb of SAYING (it frames a turn of its own: it is a turn's `frame`). The 2Sa 17:14 clause "and the LORD had ordained (צִוָּה) to frustrate ..." is also a turn's frame in the built graph, which is why the builder made it a speech. But its "content" is an infinitive clause (a purpose to act, not a quotation), and it stands after the speech ended.

A clause framing a turn whose content is a FINITE quotation is a speech. Leave it. A clause framing a "turn" whose only content is an infinitive construct is not a speech. Also, only a clause OUTSIDE the preceding turn's quoted content can be the narrator. A clause that is a part of a turn's quotation is speech.
