# Contested doctrines, as test cases for whether the graph takes sides
#
# The question this file asks is not "which reading is right". It is the test
# already the project's own test of neutrality:
#
#     Could two competent people using this graph disagree about something
#     and both find their position in it?
#
# A graph built from a corpus that its readers argue about has exactly two ways
# to fail. It can adjudicate -- state a conclusion one side rejects -- or it can
# be lopsided, holding one side's verses thoroughly and the other's barely, so
# that a reader searching in good faith finds a stacked deck. The second is the
# more likely failure here and the harder to notice, because it looks like
# ordinary incompleteness.
#
# SCORING, per row:
#
#   A  both sides reachable   the verses each side leans on are in the graph,
#                             cited, and a reader can find them from the same
#                             entity or passage.        <- the target
#   B  one side only          one position's verses are held and the other's
#                             are absent or unreachable. A defect, and the
#                             direction of the lean is the finding.
#   C  neither                not covered yet. Honest, and not a lean.
#   D  the graph adjudicates  an edge or a note states a conclusion one side
#                             would reject.             <- the worst outcome
#
# D outranks everything. A single D is worse than twenty Cs, because C is a gap
# and D is a claim. Anything scoring D is a bug to fix before beta, whatever
# the coverage numbers say.
#
# The verses listed are what each side actually cites, not a summary of the
# argument. Where a verse is textually disputed that is noted, because a proof
# text whose manuscript status is contested has to carry that status or the
# graph is quietly picking a side.
#
# Format: id | question | side A verses | side B verses | notes

## Trinity and the person of Christ

trinity | Is Jesus God? | John 1:1, John 20:28, Matt 1:23, Phil 2:6, Col 1:15, Heb 1:3, Heb 1:8, Titus 2:13, 1Tim 3:16, John 5:18 | John 14:28, Mark 13:32, Mark 10:18, Matt 19:17, Matt 27:46, John 20:17, John 17:3, John 5:19, John 5:30, John 14:24, Mark 14:36, 1Cor 15:28, 1Cor 11:3, Eph 1:17, Acts 2:36 | The measured split of the second column: 6 verses name the Father, 7 name God, 2 name both. Three of the God-worded ones address or name the Father in the same verse (John 17:3, Eph 1:17, John 20:17). Most of the remainder speak of Christ incarnate or exalted. Whether those describe the incarnate state or the eternal relation is the division itself, and the graph must not resolve it.

spirit_deity | Is the Holy Spirit God, and personal? | Acts 5:3-4, Matt 28:19, 2Cor 13:14, John 14:26, John 16:13 | 1Cor 2:11, Rom 8:26, John 14:16 | Acts 5:3-4 is the strongest: lying to the Holy Ghost is lying to God, in consecutive verses.

johannine_comma | Do the three bear record in heaven? | 1John 5:7 | 1John 5:8 | **The KJV adds a clause naming the Father, the Word and the Holy Ghost as three that are one, and the ASV in this repository does not have it.** The most explicit Trinity statement in the KJV is the one whose text is disputed. Carrying it without its status would discredit every other citation in the graph.

filioque | Does the Spirit proceed from the Father alone, or from the Father and the Son? | John 15:26 | John 16:7, John 14:26, Rom 8:9, Gal 4:6 | The split between East and West. John 15:26 says "proceedeth from the Father" and also "whom I will send unto you"; both clauses are in one verse.

## Creation and providence

creation_days | How long were the days? | Gen 1:5, Exod 20:11, Gen 5, Mark 10:6 | Ps 90:4, 2Pet 3:8, Gen 2:4, Heb 4:3-11 | Exodus 20:11 grounds the sabbath in six days, which is the strongest link between the reading and a practice.

hardening | Who hardened Pharaoh's heart? | Exod 4:21, Exod 7:3, Exod 9:12, Exod 14:4, Rom 9:17-18 | Exod 8:15, Exod 8:32, Exod 9:34, 1Sam 6:6 | **The corpus says both, in the same narrative**, which makes this the sharpest sovereignty-and-will test available and one the graph can hold entirely without adjudicating.

intermediate_state | What happens between death and resurrection? | Luke 23:43, 2Cor 5:8, Phil 1:23, Luke 16:22-23, Rev 6:9-11 | Eccl 9:5-6, Ps 6:5, Ps 115:17, Job 14:12, John 11:11-14, 1Thess 4:13-16 | Ecclesiastes 9:5 is Qoheleth speaking, which the graph already records as a speaker's claim rather than the corpus's -- and that distinction is exactly what this row tests.

## How to use this

Work a row by asking three things, in order:

1. **Is anything here a D?** Does an edge, a note or a description state a
   conclusion one side rejects? Fix it before anything else.
2. **Are both columns reachable?** From the entity or passage a reader would
   naturally start at, not by knowing the verse reference already.
3. **Is the lean measurable?** Count cited verses per column. A row at 9 and 1
   is a finding even when both columns are technically non-empty.

Rows touching textual disputes -- the Johannine Comma, Acts 8:37, the longer
ending of Mark -- carry a further requirement: **the disputed status has to be
visible where the verse is**, not only in a document like this one.

Second is the places a writer distinguishes his own judgment from a command, or a concession is named as one. Neither column settles inerrancy against infallibility, which is a distinction the corpus never draws.

Second is the practices other traditions count alongside them, each with a verse. **The corpus never enumerates a set**, so both columns are readers' lists and the row records that rather than a number.

church_governance | Who leads a congregation, and how are they set in? | Acts 14:23, Titus 1:5, 1Tim 3:1-7, 1Pet 5:1-2, Acts 20:17, Acts 20:28 | Acts 6:3, Acts 15:22, Acts 1:23-26, 1Cor 5:4, Matt 18:17 | First column is appointment from above -- elders ordained, bishops qualified. Second is the congregation choosing or acting as a body. Titus 1:5 and 1Pet 5:1-2 use elder and oversight of the same people, which is the textual fact the three-way argument runs through.

apostolic_succession | Does apostolic authority continue, and how? | Acts 1:20-26, 2Tim 2:2, Titus 1:5, Acts 14:23, 1Tim 4:14, 2Tim 1:6 | Acts 1:21-22, 1Cor 9:1, 1Cor 15:8, Eph 2:20, Rev 21:14 | First column is the passing on of office and the laying on of hands. Second is the verses attaching apostleship to conditions that cannot recur -- having seen the Lord, having been with him from the baptism of John, being the foundation already laid.
