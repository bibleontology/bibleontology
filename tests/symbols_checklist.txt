# HOW TO SCORE IT
#
# Every item falls into one of three classes, and each has a different right
# answer. A run that treats the list as 100 things to capture has failed before
# it starts.
#
#   A. The text states the key.  Bronze serpent (John 3:14), manna (John
#      6:32-35), vine (John 15:1), Hagar (Gal 4:24). Expected: a `signifies`
#      edge. Missing one is a RECALL failure.
#
#   B. The text states a comparison, not an identity.  The Spirit descends
#      "like a dove" (Matt 3:16); hope is "as an anchor of the soul" (Heb
#      6:19); "all flesh is as grass" (1Pet 1:24). Expected: `likened_to` with
#      the ground where the verse gives one. Recording these as `signifies`
#      is a PRECISION failure -- it upgrades a simile into a meaning.
#
#   C. The reading is traditional and the text does not supply it.  The fish
#      as a confession is post-biblical; menorah and shekinah are not KJV words
#      at all; "capstone" is the head stone of the corner. Expected: NOTHING,
#      or at most a lexical link. Producing a key here is the worst failure of
#      the three, because it is the graph inventing meaning and it will look
#      right to a reader who already believes it.
#
# So the run reports two numbers, and the second matters more:
#   recall    -- class A items with the key the text supplies
#   restraint -- class B and C items the graph did NOT overstate
#
# Classify each item by reading its verses before scoring, not after. Deciding
# the class once the graph's answer is visible is how a test stops testing.
#

# FLAGGED SEPARATELY: father and son
#
# Added on the owner's nomination, and they do not belong in the hundred above
# because they fail differently and harder. Every other item is a noun that is
# sometimes literal and sometimes figurative. These two are **the vocabulary
# the graph's own core is built out of**: `father_of` is its commonest relation
# type, 757 edges, and "father" runs 970 verses while "son" runs 1,799.
#
# The senses the corpus actually uses, all of them at once:
#
#   biological      "Abraham begat Isaac"                    -- `father_of`
#   originator      "the father of such as dwell in tents"   Gen 4:20
#   ancestor        "Abraham is our father"                  John 8:39
#   adoptive/moral  "ye are of your father the devil"        John 8:44
#   of a promise    "the father of all them that believe"    Rom 4:11
#   office          "a father to Pharaoh, and lord"          Gen 45:8
#   the deity       "one is your Father, which is in heaven" Matt 23:9
#
# And John 8:39-44 runs three of those senses in six verses, deliberately, as
# the argument: Abraham is their father, and he is not, and the devil is.
#
# What makes it a test rather than a task: the graph cannot distinguish these
# by the word, and `father_of` quietly asserts the biological sense on every
# edge that uses it. Gen 4:20 and Rom 4:11 are almost certainly *wrong* if any
# extractor recorded them as `father_of`, and nothing in the validator can see
# it -- both cite real verses naming both parties.
#
# **Score it by auditing the 757 existing edges**, not by adding more. The
# question is how many already mean something other than biological descent.
#
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
# RUN, 18 August 2026. The audit is done and the framing above was wrong in
# one important way.
#
# **`father_of` is a relation, not an entity, and so is every sense of the
# word.** X is father *of* Y, always -- including in the similes, where "as a
# father pitieth his children" (Ps 103:13) compares two *relations* and not a
# thing. 80% of the 1,126 occurrences sit in an explicit relational frame and
# the rest imply one. So the seven senses are seven relation types, never seven
# records, and nothing here needs an entity at all.
#
# That also draws the line against the images already minted: `father` is
# definitionally relational -- you cannot be one without a child -- where
# `shepherd` names a person doing a job and `grass` names a thing. Three
# classes, and only the third belongs in the image type.
#
# **Precision: clean. 757 edges, zero misuse.** The one hit was a false
# positive of the detector -- "Lamech is the father of Jubal" citing Gen 4:21,
# which is biological, and merely happens to sit in a verse that also calls
# Jubal the father of harpists.
#
# **Recall: the other senses are absent entirely**, which is the real finding
# and is the Nehemiah 3 problem again. Jabal, Jubal and Tubal-cain carried only
# kinship edges: the graph knew whose sons they were and not the one thing
# Genesis names them for. Nothing cited Romans 4 at all.
#
# Fixed the way that finding is always fixed -- the record says why the man is
# named -- and *not* with a new relation type. Two verses do not earn one by
# this project's own measured standard, where `warned` got in at 26.
