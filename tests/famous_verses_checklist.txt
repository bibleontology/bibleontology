# The verses readers arrive already knowing
#
# Three hundred references in four tiers, ordered by how often they are
# quoted outside the text -- on walls, in sermons, in messages between
# people who are not otherwise reading scripture that day.
#
# **The question is not whether these verses are in the graph.** Every
# verse is: the corpus is complete and the passage view will show any of
# them. The question is whether the graph has anything to say when a
# reader arrives at one, and whether what it says is worth their time.
#
# That is a harder audience than one meeting a passage cold. Each of
# these comes with a reader who already knows it, often by heart, and
# they will notice immediately if the tool has less to offer than they
# brought with them.
#
# SCORING, per reference:
#
#   cited      some relation in the graph cites at least one of its
#              verses -- the graph has *something* here
#   uncited    nothing cites it. Not necessarily wrong: some of these
#              are single sentences with no two parties to relate.
#
# `cited` is a floor and not a pass. A verse can be cited by one
# incidental edge and still have nothing a reader would want. Working a
# tier means opening each one and asking what the panel shows.
#
# Deliberately not a measure of quality. It is a worklist ordered by how
# many people will land on each entry.
