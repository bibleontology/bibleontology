# About: 1 Samuel 16:21 replaces John 3:16, reviewed (2026-10-20, evening)

**Why it changed.** The committee's third sitting (D2), as Doug ruled: *"all as recommended, D2(a)"*. The Berean
prints John 3:16 inside Jesus's words to Nicodemus (3:10-21). Where those words end is a crux, so "in the Gospel's own
voice" took a side. The worked example of a statement made outright became a plain narrative clause: 1 Sam 16:21,
"Saul loved him very much", the verb a qal wayyiqtol, וַיֶּאֱהָבֵהוּ.

**What changed on the page:**
- About's picture, from the word to the cards;
- the "Stated" card among the four standings;
- two tile examples;
- the paragraph "What the text says.";
- the welcome pop-up's picture.

`tests/test_about_primer.py` holds the new example to its standing, and also fails if John 3:16 is ever stated in the
narrator's voice again.

## Pass 1: eleven seats, Claude Haiku

**Ten seats accepted.** No seat found the verb between two men, or the choice of Saul and David, inviting a reading it
objects to. The verse makes a claim about this clause only, not about the later arc of 18:8-9.

**The rabbi's objection.** He objected narrowly to the lemma, expecting the dictionary form אָהַב (patah). The page
shows אָהֵב (tsere).
- Checked: MACULA's lemma is אָהֵב (for example, in Gen 22), and STEP Bible's TBESH lexicon gives H0157 as אָהֵב.
- **Kept**, because the page follows its source ("the original arbitrates"; MACULA decides).

**Passed to the next panels.** The panel's note on cards downstream (David's card and the love-word card, with 1 Sam
18:1-3 and 2 Sam 1:26) is a matter for those cards' own reviews, not this wording.

## Pass 2: blind second reader, Claude Sonnet

**Verdict: REVISE, minor.** The facts hold, and no seat blocks. Three fixes, all applied:

1. **"he entered his service" becomes "entered his service".** This quotes the Berean exactly; the Hebrew behind it
   is "stood before him". This was the rabbi's concern: the Berean's gloss had been quoted as if it were the text.
2. **"1 Samuel 16:21 is four clauses" becomes "In the Hebrew, 1 Samuel 16:21 is four clauses".** In the Berean's
   English, the first two clauses merge into one.
3. **"an act of feeling" becomes "a feeling".** אהב is stative, so "act" is the wrong word. This was the secular
   academic's point.

**As shipped:** eleven seats accept, the facts are checked against the Hebrew, and nothing takes a side.
