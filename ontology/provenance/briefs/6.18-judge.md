# Is this a story told again? Judge it by its beats

A Bible study site shows where one story in the Bible tells again the beats
of an earlier one -- a Gospel or Acts story and an Old Testament one, or two
Old Testament stories -- and lets readers draw their own conclusions. Each
pair below was proposed by a first reader or from a list of echoes readers
commonly cite. Neither is evidence. You are the strict judge: readers will
see your beats and explanation, and a critical reviewer will read both as an
evangelical Baptist, a Calvinist, an Arminian, a Lutheran, a Methodist, a
Charismatic, an Eastern Orthodox reader and a secular academic scholar.

Each item: `id`, `later_title`, `later_passage`, `later_text`,
`earlier_title`, `earlier_passage`, `earlier_text` (the Berean Standard
Bible, verse by verse, with references; `truncated` if a passage was cut at
80 verses), and `proposed_beats` (notes only).

## Decide

- `keep` -- at least THREE beats, each a happening in both stories, each
  quoted in the exact words of BOTH texts, and together telling the same
  sequence. The standard: John 6:9 "five barley loaves" / 2 Kgs 4:42 "twenty
  loaves of barley bread"; John 6:9 "what difference will these make among so
  many?" / 2 Kgs 4:43 "How am I to set twenty loaves before a hundred men?";
  John 6:13 "the pieces of the five barley loaves left over" / 2 Kgs 4:44
  "they ate and had some left over".
- `named` -- the later text itself names the earlier story or person and
  draws the comparison (Luke 4:25-26 names Elijah and the widow of Zarephath;
  John 3:14 names Moses and the serpent; Matt 12:40 names Jonah). Give the
  naming words as the first beat, then any further beats both texts share.
- `weak` -- a real correspondence with only two beats, or beats that hold
  only in paraphrase. Kept for the site owner to see, not shown to readers.
- `drop` -- a shared theme, a shared word, a retelling of the same event, or
  beats you cannot quote from both texts.

Hard rules:
- Every quoted beat must be copied character for character from
  `later_text` or `earlier_text`, with the verse it is in. A short exact
  phrase is better than a long one. A machine checks every quotation; one
  that is not found sinks the item.
- A beat is a happening (someone brings, asks, is raised, is taken up,
  crosses, flees), not a theme or an idea.
- The site's voice never says the later story "fulfils", "foreshadows",
  "re-enacts" or "is modelled on" the earlier one, never that one person
  "is" or "is like" another, and never what an author meant. It says what
  happens in each, side by side; the reader draws the conclusion.
- Never claim, and never deny, that a writer meant one story to recall the
  other: whether it is intentional is not known, and the site says so in its
  own line beside every echo. Unless the text itself names the earlier story
  (`named`), do not write "recalls", "echoes", "alludes to" or "draws on" --
  write what happens in each.
- Say where the stories DIFFER in a way a reader would want to know -- the
  same standard of honesty as the beats.

## Output

For packet `S2_NN.json` write `J2_NN.json` in the same folder: a JSON list,
one entry per item, same order:

```json
[{"id": 0, "verdict": "keep|named|weak|drop",
  "beats": [[["<later ref>", "<exact later words>"], ["<earlier ref>", "<exact earlier words>"]]],
  "differs": "<one sentence on a difference worth knowing, or null>",
  "explanation": "<2-3 sentences for readers: what happens in each, quoting only the Berean exactly; null if drop>",
  "why": "<one sentence for the site owner: why this verdict>"}]
```

Valid JSON (check it parses); count the entries. Write no other files.
