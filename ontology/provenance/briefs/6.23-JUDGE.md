# Judging the history books' links to each other

Each item in your packet gives:
- a passage from Genesis–Esther, with one verse either side, the verses in
  question marked `>>`;
- an earlier passage it is said to be tied to, the same way;
- the kind or kinds earlier readers proposed, and the words they matched.

All text is the Berean Standard Bible. Read **every word of both passages**
for every item. Do not judge from the readers' words.

## The question

Is the later passage tied to the earlier one in one of these ways? Decide
which (you may change the readers' kind):

- `back`: it **names or retells an earlier happening** the earlier passage
  tells, with a detail from that account ("another famine in the land,
  subsequent to the one that had occurred in Abraham's time", Gen 26:1 / Gen
  12:10; Joshua retelling the exodus, Josh 24).
- `fulfilled`: it **says something happened as an earlier word said**, and
  the earlier passage is that word -- a curse, a prophecy, a promise
  ("according to the word that the LORD had spoken through Joshua son of
  Nun", 1 Kgs 16:34 / Josh 6:26).
- `carried_out`: it **does what an earlier instruction commanded**, said in
  so many words ("just as the LORD had commanded Moses") or in the same
  details -- the tabernacle made to the instructions of Exodus 25–31, the
  priests ordained as Exodus 29 directed. Kept apart from `fulfilled`: a
  command carried out is not a word foretold.
- `quotes`: it **quotes an earlier passage's words**, often "as it is
  written in the Law of Moses" (2 Kgs 14:6 / Deut 24:16).
- `same_subject`: the same person, place or theme, but not this tie.
- `no`: not tied as claimed.

Not a link: a parallel telling of the same event in another book
(Chronicles and Samuel–Kings); a passage pointing within its own account; a
name alone. Answer `no` for these, with the reason.

Be strict. A shared word or person is not a link.

## What you must give for `back`, `fulfilled`, `carried_out` and `quotes`

`pairs`: one or more pairs of quotations, each **copied exactly**, the later
passage's words beside the earlier passage's words they match:

```json
["according to the word that the LORD had spoken through Joshua son of Nun", "1 Kings 16:34",
 "Cursed before the LORD is the man who rises up and rebuilds this city, Jericho", "Joshua 6:26"]
```

A machine checks every quotation against the verse you give.

`why`: one sentence in plain words saying how the two connect, in the
texts' own terms. No interpretation: not what it means or why it was
written.

`disputed`: true if the later words could be tied to more than one earlier
passage and the texts do not settle which, with `other` naming the rest.

## Output

Write a JSON array to the path you are given, one object per item, in order:

```json
[{"item": 12, "verdict": "fulfilled", "pairs": [...], "why": "...",
  "disputed": false, "other": null, "reason": "one line, for the record"}]
```

Reply with one line when done: how many of each verdict.
