# Five side-by-side tables for Worship and Presence: choosing and drafting the rows

A Bible website's theme pages each lead to one table that sets side by side
what different books say of one thing, in the texts' own words, with what
is alike and what differs. You are drafting ONE of these five (your prompt
says which):

- **Sacrifice -- "The offerings side by side"**: the words the texts use of
  offering and sacrifice, wherever more than one passage shares them: "a
  pleasing aroma", "without blemish", the burnt offering, the sin offering,
  the blood, the Passover lamb, "I desire mercy, not sacrifice", "obedience
  is better than sacrifice", "a broken spirit", "once for all", "living
  sacrifices", "a sacrifice of praise", and the like.
- **Priesthood -- "The priests side by side"**: the words the texts use of
  priests and priesthood: Aaron and his sons, the Levites, "minister before
  the LORD", the high priest entering once a year, "the lips of a priest",
  "a kingdom of priests", "a royal priesthood", "priests to His God", and
  the like.
- **Temple & Presence -- "The dwelling side by side"**: the words the texts
  use of God's dwelling and presence: "I will dwell among", the glory of
  the LORD filling the house, the cloud, "a dwelling for His Name", "a house
  of prayer for all the nations", "Destroy this temple", "a temple of the
  Holy Spirit", "in His holy temple", "the courts of the LORD", and the like.
- **Spirit -- "The Spirit side by side"**: the words the texts use of the
  Spirit: "the Spirit of God was hovering", "the Spirit of the LORD came
  upon", "filled with the Spirit", "I will pour out My Spirit", "a new
  spirit", "take not Your Holy Spirit from me", "baptize with the Holy
  Spirit", "the Helper", "the fruit of the Spirit", "walk by the Spirit",
  and the like.
- **Church -- "The church side by side"**: the words the texts use of the
  church and the people gathered: "the body of Christ" and "one body",
  "each member belongs to one another", "God's household", "the church of
  God",
  "a chosen people, a royal priesthood, a holy nation", "the head of the
  church", "on this rock", "pillar and foundation of the truth", and the
  like.

Your input file (named in your prompt) has: (A) the passages the site's
theme map cites for the theme; (B) the Berean editors' own notes of where a
passage is quoted, with both verses; (C) shared wording judged earlier; (D)
every verse with the theme's key words; and the titles of rows already on
the site's other tables. The whole Berean is in `bsb.txt`, one verse per
line; search it for anything more. **Look every quotation up there and copy
it exactly; never quote from memory.**

The table has these columns:
**The Law & history | Psalms & wisdom | The Prophets | Gospels & Acts | The
letters | Revelation** ("The Law & history" is Genesis to Esther; "Psalms &
wisdom" is Job to the Song of Songs; "The Prophets" is Isaiah to Malachi.)

## Choose the rows

Between 12 and 30 rows. A row is one thing said in more than one source **in
shared or closely matching words**; a shared idea with no shared wording is
not a row. A row needs at least two columns. Leave out nothing that uses the
row's own words: a place that uses them of something else may be kept as an
aside (see below).

**One home for each row.** The rows already on the site's other tables
(shown or drafted) are listed at the end of your input. Do not repeat one.
If your row would overlap one, make your row only of what that row lacks,
or leave it out; then name the overlap in `overlaps` (below), so the verses
can be moved.

## For each row, write

- `title`: a few plain words naming what the row shows, in the texts' own
  terms.
- `cells`: for each column that has it, the verses and **the words that
  carry it**, copied exactly: `{"The Law & history": [["Leviticus 1:9",
  "a pleasing aroma to the LORD"]], ...}`. Short spans. A place that uses
  the row's words of something else is kept with a third value:
  `["Proverbs 3:18", "She is a tree of life", "aside"]` -- **only for
  another subject entirely**, never for a neighbouring part of this theme.
- `alike`: one or two sentences saying what the passages share, quoting them.
- `different`: one or two sentences saying where the wording differs -- who
  speaks, to whom, what is added or left out. Quote the words that differ.
  If nothing differs worth saying, `""`.
- `overlaps` (only if any): the title of a row on another table this one
  touches, and the verses it shares.

## The rule that governs every word

**No interpretation, of any kind.** Never say what anything means, whether
one passage looks forward to, fulfils or echoes another, or why it was
written. Where a passage itself says "as it is written" or "to fulfill",
those are its words, in its cell; the Alike and Different lines never say
them in their own voice. These five tables touch the questions on which
Christian traditions most plainly part, and the site takes no side on any:

- **Sacrifice:** how a sacrifice takes away sin (substitution, ransom,
  cleansing, victory -- no line names or implies a model); whether the
  Lord's Supper is a sacrifice -- **no row sets the Supper beside the
  offerings** except where a verse's own words do; whether sacrifices are
  offered again (Ezekiel 40-48); and no line calls the offerings of the Law
  a "shadow", "type" or "picture" except in a verse's own words, in its
  cell.
- **Priesthood:** the priesthood of all believers and an ordained
  priesthood -- **no line says the church's ministers are, or are not,
  priests**; succession; and who the priest "in the order of Melchizedek"
  is, beyond what each verse says.
- **Temple & Presence:** whether a temple will be built again; and how the
  temple, Jesus' body ("Jesus was speaking about the temple of His body"),
  the church and a believer's body relate -- **report each verse's words;
  never say one is the other**.
- **Spirit:** whether the gifts of the Spirit continue today or ceased;
  whether being baptized or filled with the Spirit is a later experience
  or comes at conversion; from whom the Spirit proceeds (John 15:26's
  words, and only its words); and whether the Spirit is a person -- **no
  line calls the Spirit "He", "a person", "a force" or "power" in its own
  voice**; quote the verses.
- **Church:** who or what "this rock" is (Matthew 16:18) -- never say;
  the church and Israel; how the church is ordered (bishops, elders,
  deacons: quote, never rank); the visible and invisible church; and the
  number of the sacraments.

Every reader -- Lutheran, Reformed, Wesleyan, Baptist (often
dispensational), Pentecostal, Catholic, Orthodox, a secular scholar (and
Jewish readers of the Hebrew Bible, for whom the offerings, the priests
and the temple are their own), a biblical-theological reader -- must be
able to accept every sentence, because every sentence only reports what the
texts say.

Every quotation is checked by machine against the verse you give.

## Output

Write a JSON array to the path you are given, one object per row:

```json
[{"title": "A pleasing aroma",
  "cells": {"The Law & history": [["Leviticus 1:9", "a pleasing aroma to the LORD"]],
            "The letters": [["Ephesians 5:2", "a fragrant sacrificial offering to God"]]},
  "alike": "...", "different": "..."}]
```

Reply with one line: how many rows.
