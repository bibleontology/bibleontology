# What kind of link is this?

A Bible study site has found 317 places where a New Testament verse and an Old
Testament verse share a two-word phrase in the original Hebrew/Greek, and two
earlier readers judged that the NT verse takes up the OT verse's words. Some
truly do. Others only share an image or a set expression, or tell a similar
story. The site must say which, with the reasoning, because readers will see
your explanation.

Each item: `id`, `nt`, `ot`, `shared_words`, both verses' Berean Standard Bible
text, two verses of context each side (`nt_context`, `ot_context`), and
`editors_link_nt_verse_to` -- where the Berean editors' own quotation notes say
the NT verse quotes or points (may be empty, may name a different OT verse).

## Decide the kind

- `words_taken_up` -- the NT verse quotes or closely reproduces THIS OT verse's
  wording. Strong signs: an introducing formula ("it is written", "He said to
  Abraham"); near-identical wording; the NT book quoting the same OT verse
  outright elsewhere (say where, e.g. Hebrews quotes Ps 110:1 at 1:13).
- `shared_image` -- both use the same image or set expression ("a beast with ten
  horns", "a footstool for your feet", "myriads of holy ones"), but nothing
  shows the NT verse pointing to THIS verse rather than sharing the image. Give
  `node`: a short name for the image as the text words it.
- `story_echo` -- the NT passage tells a story whose beats match an OT story
  (a man brings barley loaves; "give it to the people to eat"; "how can I set
  this before a hundred men?"; "they will eat and have some left over"). Give
  `beats`: a list of [[NT ref, exact words], [OT ref, exact words]] pairs.
- `drop` -- the link misleads: the NT verse names or quotes a different source
  (Jude 1:14 quotes "Enoch, the seventh from Adam", not Deuteronomy), or the
  echo plainly belongs to another OT verse, or the shared words are incidental.

## Write `explanation` (shown to readers)

Two to four sentences, in the depth of this example:

> Hebrews quotes Ps 110:1 outright at 1:13 -- "Sit at My right hand until I make
> Your enemies a footstool for Your feet" -- and returns to the same words here:
> "He waits for His enemies to be made a footstool for His feet." A sustained use
> of that one verse, not a passing image.

Rules -- a second, critical reviewer will read every explanation against the
verses and as an evangelical Baptist, a Calvinist, an Arminian, a Lutheran, a
Methodist, a Charismatic, an Eastern Orthodox reader and a secular academic
scholar would, and returns any one of them would object to. Write so it passes:

- Quote only the Berean's words, exactly, in quotation marks, with the verse.
- Cite only Bible verses as evidence. Name a source only where the text itself
  names it (Jude names Enoch). No commentators, no traditions, no dates.
- Say what the texts do, never what an author "intended" or what it "fulfils"
  or "foreshadows" in the site's own voice. "Matthew introduces it with 'to
  fulfill what was spoken'" is fine -- that is the text's own claim, quoted.
- Daniel and Revelation, and any prophecy: state only what each text says. Both
  have a beast with ten horns; both say the horns are ten kings (Dan 7:24; Rev
  17:12). Never say they are the same beast, never apply either to events.
- For `shared_image`, do not say one points to the other; say both use it.

## Output

For each `sort_NN.json`, write `O_NN.json` in the same folder: a JSON list, one
entry per item, same order:

```json
[{"id": 0, "nt": "...", "ot": "...", "kind": "words_taken_up|shared_image|story_echo|drop",
  "node": "<shared_image only, else null>",
  "beats": [[["<NT ref>", "<exact words>"], ["<OT ref>", "<exact words>"]]],
  "explanation": "<2-4 sentences>"}]
```

`beats` only for `story_echo` (else []). Valid JSON; count the entries. Write no
other files.
