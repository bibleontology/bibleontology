# The new creation side by side: choosing and drafting its rows

A Bible website's "New Creation" theme page sets side by side what the
books say about a creation made new: new heavens and a new earth, death
and tears removed, God dwelling with His people, the river and the tree of
life, creation set free, a new heart and spirit, bodies raised. One table,
by source, in the texts' own words, with what is alike and what differs.

You are given `input.md`: the passages the site's theme map already cites
for New Creation, concept by concept, and seven rows the site's Apocalyptic
table already has that touch the subject (reviewed; reuse them, adapted to
these columns, wherever they fit). The whole Berean Standard Bible is in
`bsb.txt`, one verse per line ("Isaiah 65:17 For behold, I will create..."). **Look every
quotation up there and copy it exactly; never quote from memory.**

The table has these columns, by source:
**Genesis | The Prophets | Psalms & elsewhere | Gospels & Acts | The letters |
Revelation** ("The Prophets" is Isaiah to Malachi; "Psalms & elsewhere" is
any other Old Testament book.)

## Choose the rows

Between 15 and 30 rows. A row is one thing the texts describe in more than
one source, **in shared or closely matching words**: "new heavens and a new
earth" (Isa 65:17, 66:22; 2 Pet 3:13; Rev 21:1); "wipe away every tear"
(Isa 25:8; Rev 21:4); the tree of life (Gen 2:9; Rev 22:2). A shared idea
with no shared wording is not a row. A row needs at least two columns.

**The new creation of a person as well as of the world.** The texts also
speak of a person made new: "if anyone is in Christ, he is a new creation"
(2 Cor 5:17), "what counts is a new creation" (Gal 6:15), "a new heart and
a new spirit" (Ezek 36:26; Ps 51:10 "Create in me a clean heart"), "the new
self" (Eph 4:24; Col 3:10). Give these their own rows, beside the rows about
the heavens and the earth, wherever the wording is shared. Do not say how
the two relate, or whether either is now or still to come.
Genesis belongs where the later texts use its words (the tree of life, the
curse, the river), never because a reader might connect them.

## For each row, write

- `title`: a few plain words naming what the row shows, in the texts' own
  terms ("No more death or mourning", not "The defeat of death").
- `cells`: for each column that has it, the verses and **the words that
  carry it**, copied exactly: `{"The Prophets": [["Isaiah 65:17", "I will
  create new heavens and a new earth"]], ...}`. Short spans. Leave out a
  column that does not have it.
- `alike`: one or two sentences saying what the passages share, in their
  own words, quoting them.
- `different`: one or two sentences saying where their wording differs --
  a detail one has and another lacks, who does what, past or future in the
  text's own tense. Quote the words that differ. If nothing differs worth
  saying, `""`.

## The rule that governs every word

**No interpretation, of any kind.** Never say that one passage fulfils,
completes, looks forward to, echoes or reinterprets another; never say what
anything stands for, when it happens, whether it is literal or figurative,
or whether it is present or still to come -- unless quoting a text that
says so itself. Traditions read these passages very differently (as the
renewal of this earth or its replacement, as begun now or wholly future, as
the church or Israel or both); every reader must be able to accept every
sentence, because every sentence only reports what the texts say. "Isaiah
and Revelation both say 'new heavens and a new earth'" is right. "Revelation
fulfils Isaiah's promise" is wrong.

Every quotation is checked by machine against the verse you give.

## Output

Write a JSON array to the path you are given, one object per row:

```json
[{"title": "New heavens and a new earth",
  "cells": {"The Prophets": [["Isaiah 65:17", "I will create new heavens and a new earth"]],
            "Revelation": [["Revelation 21:1", "Then I saw a new heaven and a new earth"]]},
  "alike": "...", "different": "..."}]
```

Reply with one line: how many rows.
