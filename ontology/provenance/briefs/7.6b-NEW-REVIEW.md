# Reviewing a new theme's table

A Bible website's theme pages each lead to a table setting side by side
what different books say of one thing. Your packet is from one of four:
**Father, Son and Spirit side by side**, **The armor and the struggle side
by side** (Spiritual Warfare), **Set free, side by side** (Freedom), or
**Children of God side by side**. Each row has the passages' own words in
cells (a cell marked "aside" uses the row's words of something else
entirely, and is shown with an asterisk), and two lines in the site's own
voice: **Alike** and **Different**. The site takes no position on matters
Christian traditions dispute, and adds no interpretation. The disputes:
- **Father, Son and Holy Spirit:** whether the three are one God or not.
  The table says neither. No line, title or pairing may set a verse of
  oneness to answer a verse of distinction, or the reverse; the word
  "Trinity" appears in no title or line.
- **Spiritual Warfare:** whether "the satan" of Job and Zechariah is a
  title or a name; demons and their casting out today; territorial spirits;
  what the armor is.
- **Freedom:** freedom from the Law; free will; freedom as political or
  inward.
- **Children of God:** who God's children are (all people, Israel,
  believers); Israel and the church; whether "born of water and the
  Spirit" speaks of baptism.

For every row, check both lines -- and the title -- two ways.

1. **Against the texts.** Is every statement found in the cells' verses
   (the packet gives each verse in full)? Is every quotation word for word?
   Is any name, place or setting added that the cells' verses do not give?
   Does the title say more than its verses? **Watch the pairings most
   closely**: a row can take a side with every line in quotation, by
   setting two verses so that one answers the other, or so that one names
   who the other is about.

2. **As nine readers would take it.** Each judges within the 66 books all of
   them share, a constraint they agree to:
   - a **Lutheran** (law and gospel; baptism and the new birth)
   - a **Reformed** Christian (election; adoption; covenant)
   - a **Wesleyan** -- Methodist or Arminian (free will; holiness; the
     flesh and the Spirit)
   - a **Baptist / free church** Christian (believer's baptism; often
     dispensational: Israel and the church distinct)
   - a **Pentecostal / Charismatic** Christian (deliverance and the gifts
     today)
   - a **secular academic** scholar of the Bible (does the text say this?
     -- with Jewish readers of the Hebrew Bible in mind, for whom "the LORD
     is One" and "Israel is My firstborn son" are their own; and readers
     who do not hold the doctrine of the Trinity)
   - a **Catholic** (Scripture read with the Church's tradition; baptism and
     the new birth)
   - an **Eastern Orthodox** Christian (Scripture read with the Church's
     tradition; the Spirit proceeding from the Father)
   - a **biblical-theological (literary)** reader (the Bible as one story;
     the Old Testament heard on its own terms -- reading of the kind taught
     by BibleProject, Wheaton College Graduate School and Regent College,
     Vancouver)

   Would any of them object that a line, or the title, takes a side?

Verdicts, per row:
- `accept`: both lines and the title true to the texts, and all nine could
  accept them.
- `revise`: give the revised `alike` and/or `different` -- the smallest
  change that fixes it, quoting only the cells' verses -- and put any title
  change in `objections`. Name who objected and why.
- `withdraw`: the row cannot be stated neutrally.

Write a JSON array to the path you are given:

```json
[{"row": 3, "verdict": "accept", "alike": null, "different": null, "objections": []},
 {"row": 9, "verdict": "revise", "alike": null, "different": "...", "objections": ["Catholic: ..."]}]
```

Reply with one line: how many accept / revise / withdraw.
