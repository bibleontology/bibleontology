# Reading the poetry for references to events

You are one of sixteen readers. Your packet is part of the Psalms, Job,
Proverbs–Song of Solomon, or the poems set inside the narrative books, in the
Berean Standard Bible, every verse numbered. A psalm's title, where it has
one, is printed at the start of its verse 1, as the Berean prints it. Read
**every verse** of the packet, in order. Find every place where the poem
refers to an **event**: something the Bible narrates as happening, told in a
narrative passage.

## What counts

An event is a happening told as narrative: Genesis–Esther, the Gospels, Acts,
and the narrative parts of other books (Daniel 1–6, Jonah, Job 1–2 and 42).
Creation, the flood, the exodus, the giving of the law, the wilderness, the
conquest, the judges, Saul and David, the kings, the fall of Jerusalem and the
exile all count.

A poem refers to an event in one of five ways:

- **title** — its title places the poem at an event: Psalm 3, "when he fled
  from his son Absalom" (2 Samuel 15); Psalm 51, "When Nathan the prophet came
  to him after his adultery with Bathsheba" (2 Samuel 12). A title that only
  names an author ("A Psalm of David", "A Prayer of Moses") is **not** an
  event.
- **retells** — it tells the event over, with its details: Psalm 78 and 105
  on the plagues and the wilderness; Psalm 136 on Sihon and Og; the Song of
  the Sea (Exodus 15) on the crossing; the Song of Deborah (Judges 5) on the
  battle of Judges 4.
- **names** — it names the people or place and what was done: "Moses and
  Aaron were among His priests ... He spoke to them from the pillar of cloud"
  (Psalm 99:6–7); "Phinehas stood up and intervened" (Psalm 106:30).
- **occasion** — the poem names its own occasion inside the poem: "By the
  rivers of Babylon we sat and wept when we remembered Zion" (Psalm 137:1),
  the exile.
- **content** — the content suggests an occasion that the text does not
  name: Psalm 74 and 79, a sanctuary burned, with no title or verse saying
  which. List these too; they are kept apart and held.

## What does not count

- Words that a later passage takes up, or that look forward to a later event
  ("They pierced my hands and feet", "Sit at My right hand"). Those are
  another part of the work, not this one; do not list them.
- God's acts described in general, with no detail from an account: "He
  rescued me", "He made the heavens", "He does wonders". (A named detail --
  "He divided the sea", "He struck the firstborn of Egypt" -- does count.)
- Job's, Proverbs' and Ecclesiastes' teaching and images, unless they point
  at a happening the narrative tells.
- The narrative chapters of Job (1–2 and 42:7–17) are themselves an event,
  not a reference to one; references inside the speeches count.

When unsure, include it and say so in `note`; the judges who follow decide.
Missing a reference is worse than listing a doubtful one.

## Neutrality

Name what the text names. Do not explain what an event means, and do not
supply an occasion the text does not give. Where readers and traditions
dispute **which** event a poem refers to (which destruction of the sanctuary,
for instance), list it with `"disputed": true` and every candidate passage --
do not choose.

## Output

Write one JSON file to the path you are given, exactly this shape:

```json
{
  "chapters_read": {"Psalm 3": 1, "Psalm 4": 0, "...": 0},
  "refs": [
    {
      "poem": "Psalm 3:1",
      "poem_end": "Psalm 3:1",
      "quote": "when he fled from his son Absalom",
      "event": "David flees from Absalom",
      "passages": ["2 Samuel 15:13-17"],
      "listed_event": "Absalom's revolt",
      "how": "title",
      "disputed": false,
      "note": ""
    }
  ]
}
```

- `chapters_read`: **every** chapter in your packet, with how many references
  you found in it (0 is a valid answer). A chapter missing from this map means
  you did not read it.
- `quote`: the poem's own words, **copied exactly** from the packet (a machine
  checks every one). Shortest span that shows the reference.
- `passages`: the narrative passage(s) that tell the event, as references.
- `listed_event`: the matching title from `events.txt` if one fits, else null.
- `how`: `title`, `retells`, `names`, `occasion` or `content`, as above.
- One entry per reference. A psalm that retells ten events is ten entries;
  give each its own quote.

When done, reply with one line: how many references, and how many chapters.
