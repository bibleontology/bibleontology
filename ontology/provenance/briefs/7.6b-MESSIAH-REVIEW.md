# Reviewing the titles side by side

A Bible website's "Messiah" theme page -- meant to be one of the site's
front doors -- has a table of the titles the texts use (Anointed, Messiah,
Christ; Son of David; King of Israel; Son of Man; Son of God; Servant; a
prophet like Moses; a priest like Melchizedek; Shepherd; Branch; Lamb;
Stone; Immanuel; Holy One), with where each is used and of whom. Each row
has the passages' own words in cells, and two lines in the site's own voice:
**Alike** and **Different**. The site takes no position on matters
Christian traditions dispute, and adds no interpretation. Here above all:
who a title is used of is where readers part -- whether Isaiah's Servant is
Israel ("You are My servant, Israel", Isaiah 49:3) or one person; what "Son
of Man" means in Daniel and the Gospels; what "Son of God" says of Adam,
of Israel's king, of Jesus; whether a title said of David or Cyrus is about
anyone else.

For every row, check both lines two ways.

1. **Against the texts.** Is every statement found in the cells' verses?
   The lines may say of whom a text uses a title only as that text names
   them. Nothing added: never that a title "points to", "anticipates",
   "prefigures" or "is fulfilled in" anyone; never "messianic"; never who a
   title "really" means, whether two uses are of the same person, or what a
   title says of anyone's nature. Where a later text applies a title to
   Jesus, it is that text speaking, quoted. **Watch the contrasts most
   closely**: setting a title said of David beside one said of Jesus can
   carry a claim without one interpretive word.

2. **As nine readers would take it.** Each judges within the 66 books all of
   them share, a constraint they agree to:
   - a **Lutheran** (justification by faith alone; law and gospel; the sacraments)
   - a **Reformed** Christian (election; the perseverance of the saints)
   - a **Wesleyan** -- Methodist or Arminian (free will; that a believer can fall away)
   - a **Baptist / free church** Christian (believer's baptism; often dispensational)
   - a **Pentecostal / Charismatic** Christian (the gifts of the Spirit)
   - a **secular academic** scholar of the Bible (reads under no confession:
     does the text say this? -- and keeps in mind how Jewish readers read
     the Hebrew Bible's titles on their own terms)
   - a **Catholic** (Scripture read with the Church's tradition)
   - an **Eastern Orthodox** Christian (Scripture read with the Church's tradition)
   - a **biblical-theological (literary)** reader (the Bible as one story; its
     literary design; the Old Testament heard on its own terms before it is
     read as pointing forward -- reading of the kind taught by BibleProject,
     Wheaton College Graduate School and Regent College, Vancouver)

   Would any of them object that a line takes a side on who a title is
   about, or on what it says of anyone's nature?

Verdicts, per row:
- `accept`: both lines true to the texts, and all nine could accept them.
- `revise`: give the revised `alike` and/or `different` -- the smallest
  change that fixes it, quoting only the cells' verses. Name who objected
  and why.
- `withdraw`: the row cannot be stated neutrally.

Write a JSON array to the path you are given:

```json
[{"row": 3, "verdict": "accept", "alike": null, "different": null, "objections": []},
 {"row": 9, "verdict": "revise", "alike": null, "different": "...", "objections": ["Secular academic: ..."]}]
```

Reply with one line: how many accept / revise / withdraw.
