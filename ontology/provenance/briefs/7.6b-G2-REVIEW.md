# Reviewing a Covenant, Kingdom or Wisdom table

A Bible website's theme pages -- meant to be the site's front doors -- each
lead to a table setting side by side what different books say of one thing.
Your packet is from one of four: **The covenant words side by side**, **The
kingdom side by side**, **The commandments taken up** (the Law), or
**Wisdom's sayings side by side**. Each row has the passages' own words in
cells (a cell marked "aside" uses the row's words of something else, and is
shown with an asterisk), and two lines in the site's own voice: **Alike**
and **Different**. The site takes no position on matters Christian
traditions dispute, and adds no interpretation. The disputes here:
- **Covenant:** how the covenants relate to one another (one covenant in
  several forms, or distinct covenants in sequence); whether the covenant
  with Israel continues, and with whom the new covenant is made; whether
  the covenant at Sinai binds now; whether any later rite takes the place
  of a covenant sign (circumcision and baptism are never set side by side).
- **Kingdom:** whether the kingdom is present, future or both; how "the
  kingdom of heaven" and "the kingdom of God" relate; the thousand years of
  Revelation 20; how the kingdom relates to Israel and to the church.
- **Law:** whether and how the Law binds now; moral, ceremonial and civil
  law (never sorted); law and gospel; the Sabbath; what the apostles'
  letters say of the Law (quoted, never summed up).
- **Wisdom:** who wrote the books; whether Proverbs' sayings promise
  outcomes or describe what usually happens; whether Wisdom in Proverbs 8 is
  a person, or is Christ.

For every row, check both lines two ways.

1. **Against the texts.** Is every statement found in the cells' verses
   (the packet gives each verse in full)? Is every quotation word for word
   in those verses? Nothing added: never what anything means, whether one
   passage looks forward to, fulfils or echoes another, or why it was
   written. Where a verse itself says "as it is written" or "to fulfill",
   those words may be quoted, but the line never says it in its own voice.
   **Watch the contrasts most closely**: a "Different" line can take a side
   without one interpretive word, by what it chooses to set side by side or
   by how it describes an order or a change ("earlier", "replaced", "now").

2. **As nine readers would take it.** Each judges within the 66 books all of
   them share, a constraint they agree to:
   - a **Lutheran** (justification by faith alone; law and gospel; the sacraments)
   - a **Reformed** Christian (election; the perseverance of the saints; covenant theology)
   - a **Wesleyan** -- Methodist or Arminian (free will; that a believer can fall away)
   - a **Baptist / free church** Christian (believer's baptism; often dispensational)
   - a **Pentecostal / Charismatic** Christian (the gifts of the Spirit)
   - a **secular academic** scholar of the Bible (reads under no confession:
     does the text say this? -- and keeps Jewish readers of the Hebrew Bible
     in mind, for whom the covenant and the Law are their own)
   - a **Catholic** (Scripture read with the Church's tradition)
   - an **Eastern Orthodox** Christian (Scripture read with the Church's tradition)
   - a **biblical-theological (literary)** reader (the Bible as one story; its
     literary design; the Old Testament heard on its own terms before it is
     read as pointing forward -- reading of the kind taught by BibleProject,
     Wheaton College Graduate School and Regent College, Vancouver)

   Would any of them -- covenant or dispensational, on the Law's place now,
   on the kingdom's timing -- object that a line takes a side?

Verdicts, per row:
- `accept`: both lines true to the texts, and all nine could accept them.
- `revise`: give the revised `alike` and/or `different` -- the smallest
  change that fixes it, quoting only the cells' verses. Name who objected
  and why.
- `withdraw`: the row cannot be stated neutrally.

Write a JSON array to the path you are given:

```json
[{"row": 3, "verdict": "accept", "alike": null, "different": null, "objections": []},
 {"row": 9, "verdict": "revise", "alike": null, "different": "...", "objections": ["Secular academic: ..."]}]
```

Reply with one line: how many accept / revise / withdraw.
