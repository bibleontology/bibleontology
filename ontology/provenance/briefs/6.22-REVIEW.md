# Reviewing the sentences a Bible site would show

A website that maps the Bible's own connections shows, beside a verse of
Revelation, one plain sentence saying what it has in common with another
passage. Each item in your packet gives the Revelation verse(s), the other
passage, the link's **kind** (`words` taken up, an `image` both use, a
person or place Revelation `names`), and that sentence. The site takes no
position on matters Christian traditions dispute, and adds no
interpretation. **Revelation is where this matters most**: readers and
traditions differ widely on what its visions refer to and when.

For every item, check the sentence two ways.

1. **Against the text.** Is every statement in it found in the two passages
   given? Nothing added: never what an image stands for, whom a figure is,
   when a vision happens, that one passage fulfils or predicts the other, or
   any identification the texts do not make themselves ("the beast is ...",
   "Babylon here is ..."). A sentence may say "both describe X" or
   "Revelation names Y, whom Z tells of" -- no more.

2. **As nine readers would take it.** Each judges within the 66 books all of
   them share, a constraint they agree to:
   - a **Lutheran** (justification by faith alone; law and gospel; the sacraments)
   - a **Reformed** Christian (election; the perseverance of the saints)
   - a **Wesleyan** -- Methodist or Arminian (free will; that a believer can fall away)
   - a **Baptist / free church** Christian (believer's baptism; often dispensational)
   - a **Pentecostal / Charismatic** Christian (the gifts of the Spirit)
   - a **secular academic** scholar of the Bible (reads under no confession: does the text say this?)
   - a **Catholic** (Scripture read with the Church's tradition)
   - an **Eastern Orthodox** Christian (Scripture read with the Church's tradition)
   - a **biblical-theological (literary)** reader (the Bible as one story; its
     literary design; the Old Testament heard on its own terms before it is
     read as pointing forward -- reading of the kind taught by BibleProject,
     Wheaton College Graduate School and Regent College, Vancouver)

   Would any of them object that the sentence takes a side -- preterist,
   historicist, futurist or idealist; on the millennium, the rapture, the
   tribulation, Israel and the church -- assumes an identification they
   reject, or states as fact what is contested?

Verdicts:
- `accept`: true to both texts, and all nine could accept it.
- `revise`: give the revised sentence -- the smallest change that fixes it,
  saying only what the two texts say. Name who objected and why.
- `withdraw`: the link itself cannot be stated neutrally.

Write a JSON array to the path you are given:

```json
[{"item": 3, "verdict": "accept", "sentence": null, "objections": []},
 {"item": 9, "verdict": "revise", "sentence": "...", "objections": ["Futurist Baptist: ..."]}]
```

Reply with one line: how many accept / revise / withdraw.
