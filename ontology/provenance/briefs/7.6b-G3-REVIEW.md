# Reviewing a Worship and Presence table

A Bible website's theme pages -- meant to be the site's front doors -- each
lead to a table setting side by side what different books say of one thing.
Your packet is from one of five: **The offerings side by side**
(Sacrifice), **The priests side by side**, **The dwelling side by side**
(Temple & Presence), **The Spirit side by side**, or **The church side by
side**. Each row has the passages' own words in cells (a cell marked
"aside" uses the row's words of something else entirely, and is shown with
an asterisk), and two lines in the site's own voice: **Alike** and
**Different**. The site takes no position on matters Christian traditions
dispute, and adds no interpretation. These five touch the questions on
which traditions most plainly part:
- **Sacrifice:** how a sacrifice takes away sin (no model named or
  implied); whether the Lord's Supper is a sacrifice; whether sacrifices
  are offered again; the offerings as "shadow" or "type" (only in a
  verse's own words).
- **Priesthood:** the priesthood of all believers and an ordained
  priesthood (no line says ministers are, or are not, priests);
  succession; who the priest in the order of Melchizedek is.
- **Temple & Presence:** whether a temple will be built again; how the
  temple, Jesus' body, the church and a believer's body relate (no line
  says one is the other).
- **Spirit:** whether the gifts continue today; whether being baptized or
  filled with the Spirit is a later experience; from whom the Spirit
  proceeds; whether the Spirit is a person (no line calls the Spirit
  "He", "a person", "a force" or "power" in its own voice).
- **Church:** who or what "this rock" is; the church and Israel; how the
  church is ordered; the visible and invisible church; the number of the
  sacraments.

For every row, check both lines two ways.

1. **Against the texts.** Is every statement found in the cells' verses
   (the packet gives each verse in full)? Is every quotation word for word
   in those verses? Nothing added: never what anything means, whether one
   passage looks forward to, fulfils or echoes another, or why it was
   written. Where a verse itself says "as it is written" or "to fulfill",
   those words may be quoted, but the line never says it in its own voice.
   **Watch the contrasts most closely**: a "Different" line can take a side
   without one interpretive word, by what it chooses to set side by side or
   by how it describes an order or a change ("earlier", "replaced", "now",
   "no longer", "completed").

2. **As nine readers would take it.** Each judges within the 66 books all of
   them share, a constraint they agree to:
   - a **Lutheran** (justification by faith alone; law and gospel; the
     sacraments, the real presence)
   - a **Reformed** Christian (election; covenant theology; a spiritual
     presence in the Supper)
   - a **Wesleyan** -- Methodist or Arminian (free will; that a believer can
     fall away; holiness)
   - a **Baptist / free church** Christian (believer's baptism; the Supper
     as a memorial; the priesthood of all believers; often dispensational)
   - a **Pentecostal / Charismatic** Christian (the gifts of the Spirit
     today; baptism in the Spirit)
   - a **secular academic** scholar of the Bible (reads under no confession:
     does the text say this? -- and keeps Jewish readers of the Hebrew Bible
     in mind, for whom the offerings, the priests and the temple are their
     own)
   - a **Catholic** (Scripture read with the Church's tradition; the Mass as
     sacrifice; the ordained priesthood; Peter)
   - an **Eastern Orthodox** Christian (Scripture read with the Church's
     tradition; the Spirit proceeding from the Father)
   - a **biblical-theological (literary)** reader (the Bible as one story; its
     literary design; the Old Testament heard on its own terms before it is
     read as pointing forward -- reading of the kind taught by BibleProject,
     Wheaton College Graduate School and Regent College, Vancouver)

   Would any of them object that a line takes a side?

Verdicts, per row:
- `accept`: both lines true to the texts, and all nine could accept them.
- `revise`: give the revised `alike` and/or `different` -- the smallest
  change that fixes it, quoting only the cells' verses. Name who objected
  and why.
- `withdraw`: the row cannot be stated neutrally.

Write a JSON array to the path you are given:

```json
[{"row": 3, "verdict": "accept", "alike": null, "different": null, "objections": []},
 {"row": 9, "verdict": "revise", "alike": null, "different": "...", "objections": ["Catholic: ..."]}]
```

Reply with one line: how many accept / revise / withdraw.
