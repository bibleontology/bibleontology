# Redoing a theme's outline in the texts' own words

A Bible website maps the Bible's major themes. Each theme has a front page
(an introduction, quotations, a table setting a theme's shared wordings side
by side) and, below it, an **outline**: core concepts, the passages for each,
secondary concepts, and links to other themes. Twenty outlines came from
another reader (Microsoft Copilot) and were never checked line by line: the
concept names are often that reader's framing ("Creation as Temple-Pattern",
"Creation for Christ / Through Christ"), the notes beside passages are
glosses rather than the text, and many passages cite whole runs of chapters.
**You are redoing ONE outline** (your packet says which), so that it speaks
in the site's voice: **every concept name and every note is in the texts'
own words or a plain label, never an interpretation**, and the site takes no
side where Christian traditions part.

The whole Berean Standard Bible is in `bsb.txt`, one verse per line
("Genesis 1:1 In the beginning ..."). **Look up every verse and copy every
quotation exactly; never quote from memory.**

## What to do with Copilot's outline (section 1 of your packet)

- **Keep** a concept whose name says no more than its passages, in its
  plain wording ("Goodness of creation" may stay if the passages say
  "good").
- **Rename** a concept whose name says more than its passages, or frames
  them ("X as Y", "for Christ", "Temple-Pattern"): use the texts' own phrase
  ("Let there be light", "It was very good", "In the beginning") or a plain
  label ("The seventh day"). If no passage holds what the name claims,
  **drop** the concept, or keep its passages under a name they support.
- **Passages:** keep those that hold the concept; **cut a long range to the
  verses that carry it** (not "Job 38–41" but "Job 38:8–11"); a whole
  chapter only where the whole chapter is the point (Genesis 1). Add
  passages Copilot missed where the texts say the concept in so many words.
- **Notes:** a quotation copied exactly from the verse, in “curly quotes”,
  or nothing. No glosses ("light/dark", "Christ as ...").

## The opportunity: the sub-themes

Doug, 2026-10-08: *"I do think that this is a real opportunity to really tie
in a lot of important sub-themes."* An outline is where a theme names the
sub-themes the texts develop inside it -- under Creation, for instance, rest
on the seventh day, the waters given their bound, the work of the garden,
"one flesh", the heavens declaring. **Make the core concepts the theme's
real sub-themes**, each one the texts develop in more than one place, each
in their words. Section 4 of your packet lists the cards the site already
has in this theme's verses: **where a sub-theme is one of those cards, name
the concept as that card is named** (or close to it), so the page can link
straight to it. Do not invent a sub-theme to use a card.

Section 3 is the theme's front page and table, already published: your
outline **agrees with them**. **Overlap is welcome** (Doug, 2026-10-08:
*"overlap with the tables is not discouraged as this is a different way of
grouping"*): a table row sets one shared wording side by side across books;
a core concept gathers a sub-theme, whatever its wording. Never leave a
sub-theme out because a row or the front page already holds its verses.

## End on the theme's own words of hope

Doug, 2026-10-08: *"I would like to end each outline, even the dark ones,
with some glimmer of hope because I do believe that is what the Bible does
with each of these themes, even the dark ones like judgement and the sin
one"* -- made a rule on his card. **The last core concept is the theme's own
words of hope, from passages within the theme** (Judgment: "In wrath,
remember mercy", Habakkuk 3:2; Sin: "where sin increased, grace increased
all the more", Romans 5:20). It is an arrangement, not a claim: **no line in
the site's voice says the Bible always ends in hope**, and **nothing dark is
left out or softened to make room** -- a hard word the texts leave standing
stays a sub-theme of its own.

## Take no side

- No concept may say that an Old Testament passage is fulfilled in, points
  to, or is a type of anything in the New ("Creation for Christ"): where the
  New Testament itself says a thing ("Through Him all things were made",
  John 1:3), quote it as a passage of its own concept in its own words.
- Nothing on the age of the earth, evolution, the days of Genesis 1, or how
  literally to read them; on whether the image of God is reason, rule,
  relationship or something else; on the order of the last things.
- Jewish readers of the Hebrew Bible, a secular scholar, Catholic, Orthodox,
  Lutheran, Reformed, Wesleyan, Baptist and Pentecostal readers must each
  find every line true to the text.

## The format (it is parsed by a program: follow it exactly)

```
THEME <n> — <TITLE AS IN YOUR PACKET>
<one line: a plain subtitle>
A. Core Ontological Concepts (Primary Nodes)

1. <concept name>
2. <concept name>
...

B. Scriptural Support for Each Concept (Public‑Domain Translations)
1. <the same concept name>

* Genesis 1:3 (“Let there be light”)
* Psalm 33:6

2. <the same concept name>

* ...

C. Secondary Ontological Concepts (Sub‑Nodes / Attributes)

* <A> → <B> (Gen 2:2; Exod 20:11)

D. Suggested Ontology Linkages

* <This theme> → <Other theme>

E. Optional Expansion
```

- Use the theme number and title line from your packet (section 1's first
  line), the em dash "—", and the headings exactly as above (the "‑" in
  "Public‑Domain" and "Sub‑Nodes" is a non-breaking hyphen; copy it).
- **At least 8 core concepts, and as many as the texts develop**, each a few
  words. Themes need not be balanced (Doug, 2026-10-08: *"our themes do not
  have to be balanced either - if Image of God blows up with His
  attributes, so be it"*); never move a real sub-theme to section C to keep
  the count down. **B:** 3 to 8 passages each,
  every reference one the Berean has. **C:** 4 to 10 secondary concepts,
  "A → B (short references)", the references cut to the verses as in B.
  **D:** at least 4 links, and as many as your passages ground (Doug,
  2026-10-08, lifting the ten: *"if they are related they are related"*),
  using **only these labels**: Creation, Image of God,
  Blessing & Commission, Covenant, Kingdom, Law/Wisdom, Sacrifice,
  Priesthood, Temple/Presence, Sin/Exile/Death, Judgment, Mercy &
  Restoration, Deliverance/Salvation, Prophetic Word & Fulfillment,
  Messiah/Christology, Spirit, Church, New Creation, Wisdom Literature,
  Apocalyptic; Father, Son and Holy Spirit; Spiritual Warfare; Freedom;
  Children of God. Link only where your passages truly overlap the other
  theme's subject.
