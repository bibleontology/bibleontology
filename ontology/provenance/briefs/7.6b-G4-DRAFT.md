# Four side-by-side tables for Sin, Judgment and Restoration: choosing and drafting the rows

Read `7.6b-G3-DRAFT.md` first: its rules hold here unchanged -- the
columns, shared or closely matching words, two columns a row, cells copied
exactly from `bsb.txt` and never quoted from memory, asides only for
another subject entirely, one home for each row (the rows already on the
site's other tables are listed at the end of your input), `overlaps`, the
output form, and no interpretation of any kind. This brief gives the four
tables and their disputes. You are drafting ONE (your prompt says which).

- **Sin, Exile & Death -- "Sin, exile and death side by side"**: the words
  the texts use of sin, of exile, and of death, wherever more than one
  passage shares them: "sin is crouching at your door", "to dust you shall
  return", "The soul who sins is the one who will die", "carried away the Israelites", "by the
  rivers of Babylon", "There is no one who does good", "your iniquities
  have built barriers between you and your God", "the wages of sin is death", "dead in your
  trespasses", "the sting of death", and the like.
- **Judgment -- "Judgment side by side"**: the words the texts use of
  judgment: "the Judge of all the earth", "the day of the LORD", "the great
  and awesome Day of the LORD", "the day of wrath", "every deed into judgment",
  "judge the living and the dead", "the judgment seat", "the books were
  opened", the winepress, "eternal fire", "outer darkness", and the like.
- **Deliverance -- "Deliverance side by side"**: the words the texts use of
  being delivered and saved: "the LORD will fight for you", "a mighty hand
  and an outstretched arm", "brought you out of the land of Egypt", "The
  LORD is my light and my salvation", "my rock, my fortress, and my deliverer", "they cried out to the
  LORD", "everyone who calls on the name of the LORD will be saved", "by
  grace you have been saved", and the like. **Leave out the words of
  bondage and release** -- slave and slavery, the yoke, "set free",
  liberty, the oppressed released: they belong to a theme still to be
  built, **Freedom**, and will be drafted there.
- **Mercy & Restoration -- "Mercy and restoration side by side"**: the
  words the texts use of mercy and of restoring: "compassionate and
  gracious, slow to anger, abounding in loving devotion" (said in many
  books), "His loving devotion endures forever", "restore you from captivity",
  "I will gather you", "Return to Me, and I will return to you", "new every
  morning", "rend your hearts", "heal their land", "rich in mercy", "have
  mercy on me", and the like.

## The disputes, which the site takes no side on

- **Sin, Exile & Death:** how sin passes from the first man to others --
  inherited guilt, inherited corruption, or ancestral sin as the Orthodox
  read it -- **never say**, and quote Romans 5:12 only in its own words;
  how far human nature is fallen; whether "death" in a verse is of the
  body, of the soul, or both -- report the verse's word; and whether the
  exile has ended, or ends for Israel in the future.
- **Judgment:** what hell is -- eternal conscious punishment, destruction
  (annihilation), or restoration in the end -- quote "eternal",
  "destruction", "fire"
  only as each verse has them, and never set them so that one answers
  another; purgatory; the order and timing of the last things (a rapture,
  a millennium); and whether judgment is by works, by faith, or both --
  where verses speak of works and of faith, quote each and stop.
- **Deliverance:** how a person is saved -- by faith alone, by faith and
  works, through the sacraments -- and whether salvation can be lost;
  election and free will; whether all will be saved. Quote each verse's
  words of saving, and never sum up.
- **Mercy & Restoration:** whether all will be restored in the end; whether
  the promises of restoration to Israel are to Israel, to the church, or
  to both; and whether those promises are yet to come. Report each
  promise's own words, to whom the verse says it is spoken.

Every reader -- Lutheran, Reformed, Wesleyan, Baptist (often
dispensational), Pentecostal, Catholic, Orthodox, a secular scholar (and
Jewish readers of the Hebrew Bible), a biblical-theological reader -- must
be able to accept every sentence, because every sentence only reports what
the texts say. And **a row can take a side by its pairing alone, with
every line in quotation**: setting two verses side by side so that one
answers the other, or so that one names who the other is about. Do not.
