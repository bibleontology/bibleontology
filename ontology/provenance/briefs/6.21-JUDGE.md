# Judging references from the letters to events

Each item in your packet gives:
- a passage from a letter (Romans–Jude), with two verses either side for
  context, the verses in question marked `>>`;
- an event, by its title, and the narrative passage that tells it, in full;
- what an earlier reader said the reference is.

All text is the Berean Standard Bible. Read **every word of both texts** for
every item. Do not judge from the reader's note.

## The question

Does the letter **refer to this happening** — name it, or recount it with a
concrete detail from this account (who, where, when, what was done)?

- `refers`: yes. The letter points at this happening.
- `same_subject`: the letter speaks of the same person, place or theme, but
  does not point at this happening (e.g. it mentions Abraham's faith in
  general, not the event told here; or states a doctrine with no detail of
  the account).
- `no`: not this happening (the reader matched the wrong account, or nothing
  is referred to).

Be strict. A shared word is not a reference. A shared person is not a
reference. A detail that **belongs to this account** is.

## What you must give for `refers`

`pairs`: one or more pairs of quotations, each **copied exactly**, the
letter's words beside the account's words that they correspond to:

```json
["By faith Abel offered God a better sacrifice than Cain did", "Hebrews 11:4",
 "the LORD looked with favor on Abel and his offering", "Genesis 4:4"]
```

A machine checks every quotation against the verse you give. Shortest spans
that show the correspondence.

`why`: one sentence, in plain words, saying what the letter refers to, in
the text's own terms. **No interpretation**: not what it means, not why the
writer says it, nothing a tradition would dispute. Example: "Hebrews names
Abel's offering, which Genesis 4 tells: the LORD looked with favor on it."

`disputed`: true if the letter's details also fit another account and
readers dispute which is meant (Galatians 2:1–10 with Acts 11 or Acts 15),
with `other` naming it. Otherwise false.

## Output

Write a JSON array to the path you are given, one object per item, in the
packet's order:

```json
[{"item": 12, "verdict": "refers", "pairs": [...], "why": "...",
  "disputed": false, "other": null, "reason": "one line, for the record"}]
```

`reason` is for the record, not shown: why you decided as you did, in one
line. Reply with one line when done: how many refers / same_subject / no.
