# Judging references between the prophets and events

Each item in your packet gives:
- a passage from a prophetic book (Isaiah–Malachi), with two verses either
  side, the verses in question marked `>>`;
- its **kind**, which you judge it as and never change:
  - `back` -- the prophet names or retells an *earlier* happening;
  - `own_time` -- the text dates or places the prophecy at a happening of
    its own day that a narrative tells;
  - `forward` -- a *later* passage that tells an event takes up the
    prophet's words;
- an event, by its title, and the narrative passage that tells it (for
  `forward`, the later passage), in full;
- what an earlier reader said.

All text is the Berean Standard Bible. Read **every word of both texts** for
every item. Do not judge from the reader's note.

## The question, by kind

- `back`: does the prophet **refer to this happening** -- name it, or recount
  it with a detail from this account?
- `own_time`: do the prophet's **own words** (a king, a year, a named event,
  or a narrative told in both books) tie the passage to **this** happening?
- `forward`: does the later passage **quote the prophet's words, or say that
  this happened as the prophet spoke**? A likeness of theme is not enough.

Verdicts:
- `refers`: yes, as above.
- `suggests`: the content fits this happening, but the words do not name or
  tie it, and other happenings could fit (kept as a record, not shown).
- `same_subject`: same person, place or theme, but not this happening.
- `no`: not this happening.

Be strict. A shared word or person is not a reference.

## What you must give for `refers` and `suggests`

`pairs`: one or more pairs of quotations, each **copied exactly**, the
prophet's words beside the account's words they correspond to (for
`forward`, the later passage's words that quote or name them):

```json
["In the year that King Uzziah died", "Isaiah 6:1",
 "And Uzziah rested with his fathers", "2 Kings 15:7"]
```

A machine checks every quotation against the verse you give.

`why`: one sentence, in plain words, saying how the two connect, in the
texts' own terms. **No interpretation**: not what a prophecy means, not whom
it is about, not when it is fulfilled. For `forward`, say only what the later
text itself says, in its own words ("Matthew says this took place to fulfill
what the Lord had spoken through the prophet"). For `own_time`, say what the
dates or names place where ("Isaiah dates his vision to the year King Uzziah
died, which 2 Kings 15 tells").

`disputed`: true if the details also fit another account and readers
dispute which is meant, with `other` naming it. Otherwise false.

## Output

Write a JSON array to the path you are given, one object per item, in order:

```json
[{"item": 12, "verdict": "refers", "pairs": [...], "why": "...",
  "disputed": false, "other": null, "reason": "one line, for the record"}]
```

Reply with one line when done: how many refers / suggests / same_subject / no.
