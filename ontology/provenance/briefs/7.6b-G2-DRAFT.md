# Four side-by-side tables for Covenant, Kingdom and Wisdom: choosing and drafting the rows

A Bible website's theme pages each lead to one table that sets side by side
what different books say of one thing, in the texts' own words, with what
is alike and what differs. You are drafting ONE of these four (your prompt
says which):

- **Covenant -- "The covenant words side by side"**: the words the texts
  use of covenant, wherever more than one passage shares them: "I will
  establish My covenant", "an everlasting covenant", "the sign of the
  covenant", "I will be your God, and you will be My people", "remember My
  covenant", "keep My covenant", "broke My covenant", "the blood of the
  covenant", "a new covenant", "keeps His covenant of loving devotion", the
  covenant with David, the ark of the covenant, and the like. A row is one
  such wording, set out in every book that has it.
- **Kingdom -- "The kingdom side by side"**: the words the texts use of
  God's reign and kingdom: "The LORD reigns", "an everlasting kingdom",
  "His kingdom rules over all", "the kingdom of God" and "the kingdom of
  heaven" (each its own wording), "Your kingdom come", "the throne of His
  kingdom", "the kingdom of the world has become the kingdom of our Lord",
  "the saints will receive the kingdom", and the like.
- **Law -- "The commandments taken up"**: one row per commandment or
  saying of the Law (Exodus to Deuteronomy) that later passages quote or
  repeat in its words: "Love the LORD your God with all your heart",
  "Love your neighbor as yourself", "be holy, because I am holy", "Honor
  your father and mother", the commandments of Exodus 20 and Deuteronomy 5,
  "eye for eye", "Do not muzzle an ox", "the man who does these
  things will live by them", "Cursed is everyone who does not continue",
  "Man does not live on bread alone", and the like. The first cell is the
  Law's own words; the others are the places that take them up.
- **Wisdom -- "Wisdom's sayings side by side"**: one row per saying of
  Job, Proverbs, Ecclesiastes or the wisdom psalms that another passage
  repeats or takes up in its words: "The fear of the LORD is the beginning
  of wisdom", "Naked I came from my mother's womb", "the LORD disciplines
  the one He loves", "God opposes the proud but gives grace to the humble",
  "love covers a multitude of sins", "If your enemy is hungry, feed him",
  "a dog returns to its vomit", "Do not boast about tomorrow", "He catches
  the wise in their craftiness", man "like grass" or "a vapor", and the
  like.

Your input file (named in your prompt) has: (A) the passages the site's
theme map cites for the theme; (B) the Berean editors' own notes of where a
passage is quoted, with both verses; (C) shared wording judged earlier; (D)
every verse with the theme's key words; and the titles of rows already on
the site's other tables. The whole Berean is in `bsb.txt`, one verse per
line; search it for anything more. **Look every quotation up there and copy
it exactly; never quote from memory.**

The table has these columns:
**The Law & history | Psalms & wisdom | The Prophets | Gospels & Acts | The
letters | Revelation** ("The Law & history" is Genesis to Esther; "Psalms &
wisdom" is Job to the Song of Songs; "The Prophets" is Isaiah to Malachi.)

## Choose the rows

Between 12 and 30 rows. A row is one thing said in more than one source **in
shared or closely matching words**; a shared idea with no shared wording is
not a row. A row needs at least two columns -- or, for the Law and Wisdom
tables only, two books in different parts of the Bible even if one column
(Proverbs taken up in Psalms counts). Leave out nothing that uses the row's
own words: a place that uses them of something else may be kept as an aside
(see below).

**One home for each row.** The rows already on the site's other tables are
listed at the end of your input. Do not repeat one. If your row would
overlap one, make your row only of what that row lacks, or leave it out;
then name the overlap in `overlaps` (below), so the verses can be moved.

## For each row, write

- `title`: a few plain words naming what the row shows, in the texts' own
  terms.
- `cells`: for each column that has it, the verses and **the words that
  carry it**, copied exactly: `{"The Law & history": [["Leviticus 19:18",
  "love your neighbor as yourself"]], ...}`. Short spans. A place that uses
  the row's words of something else is kept with a third value:
  `["Proverbs 3:18", "She is a tree of life", "aside"]`.
- `alike`: one or two sentences saying what the passages share, quoting them.
- `different`: one or two sentences saying where the wording differs -- who
  speaks, to whom, what is added or left out. Quote the words that differ.
  If nothing differs worth saying, `""`.
- `overlaps` (only if any): the title of a row on another table this one
  touches, and the verses it shares.

## The rule that governs every word

**No interpretation, of any kind.** Never say what anything means, whether
one passage looks forward to, fulfils or echoes another, or why it was
written. Where a passage itself says "as it is written" or "to fulfill",
those are its words, in its cell; the Alike and Different lines never say
them in their own voice. Each table has its own disputes the site takes no
side on:

- **Covenant:** how the covenants relate to one another (one covenant in
  several forms, or distinct covenants in sequence); whether the covenant
  with Israel continues, and with whom the new covenant is made; whether
  the covenant at Sinai binds now; and whether any later rite takes the
  place of a covenant sign. **No row sets circumcision beside baptism**,
  and no line calls one covenant earlier, older, better or replaced except
  in a verse's own words, in its cell.
- **Kingdom:** whether the kingdom is present, future or both, and when it
  comes; how "the kingdom of heaven" and "the kingdom of God" relate --
  never say they are the same or different, report each verse's words; the
  thousand years of Revelation 20; and how the kingdom relates to Israel and
  to the church.
- **Law:** whether and how the Law binds now; which commandments are moral,
  ceremonial or civil -- **never sort them**; law and gospel; the Sabbath;
  and what the apostles' letters say of the Law -- quote them, never sum
  them up ("Paul sets the Law aside", "Paul upholds the Law" are both
  sides).
- **Wisdom:** who wrote the books; whether Proverbs' sayings promise
  outcomes or describe what usually happens; and whether Wisdom in
  Proverbs 8 is a person, or is Christ. No row sets Proverbs 8 beside John 1
  or 1 Corinthians 1:24 unless the words themselves match.

Every reader -- Lutheran, Reformed, Wesleyan, Baptist (often
dispensational), Pentecostal, Catholic, Orthodox, a secular scholar (and
Jewish readers of the Hebrew Bible), a biblical-theological reader -- must
be able to accept every sentence, because every sentence only reports what
the texts say.

Every quotation is checked by machine against the verse you give.

## Output

Write a JSON array to the path you are given, one object per row:

```json
[{"title": "Love your neighbor as yourself",
  "cells": {"The Law & history": [["Leviticus 19:18", "love your neighbor as yourself"]],
            "Gospels & Acts": [["Matthew 22:39", "Love your neighbor as yourself"]]},
  "alike": "...", "different": "..."}]
```

Reply with one line: how many rows.
