# Second judge: is this pattern clear, or would any two such stories share it?

A Bible study site will show readers pairs of stories whose steps match --
"a story told again" -- with the steps side by side in the text's own words,
and no claim that either writer had the other in mind. A first judge
accepted every pair below. First judges here are generous. You are the
skeptic. The site's owner: "if the pattern is clear and we don't claim
intentionality - we should be fairly safe." Your question is whether the
pattern is CLEAR.

Each item: `id`, both passages in full (`later_text`, `earlier_text`, the
Berean Standard Bible), and the first judge's `beats` (pairs of exact
quotations) and `explanation`.

Ask of each pair:
1. **Are the beats the same happenings**, or only the same kind of thing
   (both have a meal, a journey, a prayer, a death, a crowd, a king)? A beat
   that any two stories of this kind would share is not evidence.
2. **Is it a sequence?** Do the beats come in a comparable order and make up
   the body of both stories, or are they scattered details pulled from long
   passages?
3. **Is anything distinctive shared** -- a detail, a number, a turn of
   phrase, a reversal -- that a reader would not expect by chance? The
   barley loaves, "how can I set this before a hundred men?" and "left over"
   are distinctive; "a man prayed" is not.
4. **Does the explanation say only what the beats show**, with no claim that
   one story was told with the other in mind (unless the later text itself
   names the earlier one)?

**Read both full passages for every item before judging it** -- not the
beats alone, and not your memory of the story. A verdict given without
reading is worse than none. If the packet is too long to read in full, say
so in your reply instead of guessing.

Verdict:
- `clear` -- yes to 1-3: a reader shown the beats would see the same story
  shape without being told.
- `thin` -- real but rests on generic beats or one distinctive detail.
- `no` -- the likeness is the genre, not these two stories.

## Output

For packet `R3_NN.json` write `V3_NN.json` in the same folder: a JSON list,
one entry per item, same order:

```json
[{"id": 0, "verdict": "clear|thin|no",
  "distinctive": "<the detail a reader would not expect by chance, as EXACT words copied from one of the two texts (a machine checks they are there); required when clear, else null>",
  "generic": ["<beats that any two such stories would share>"],
  "explanation_ok": true,
  "why": "<one sentence>"}]
```

Valid JSON (check it parses); count the entries. Write no other files.
