# Four new themes: drafting each theme's outline

A Bible website maps the Bible's major themes. Twenty came from another
reader's outline, each in a fixed format: core concepts, the passages for
each, secondary concepts, and links to other themes. Four more are being
added, and **their outlines are the site's own**, so they speak in the site's
voice: **every concept name and every note is in the texts' own words or in
plain description, never an interpretation.** You are drafting ONE outline
(your prompt says which). The whole Berean Standard Bible is in `bsb.txt`,
one verse per line; **look up every verse and copy every quotation exactly;
never quote from memory.**

## The format (it is parsed by a program: follow it exactly)

```
THEME 22 — FATHER, SON AND HOLY SPIRIT
<one line: a plain subtitle>
A. Core Ontological Concepts (Primary Nodes)

1. <concept name>
2. <concept name>
...

B. Scriptural Support for Each Concept (Public‑Domain Translations)
1. <the same concept name>

* Matthew 28:19 (“in the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit”)
* 2 Corinthians 13:14

2. <the same concept name>

* ...

C. Secondary Ontological Concepts (Sub‑Nodes / Attributes)

* <A> → <B> (Book 1:2; Book 3:4)

D. Suggested Ontology Linkages

* <This theme> → <Other theme>

E. Optional Expansion
```

- Use the theme number and title your prompt gives, the em dash "—", and
  the section headings exactly as above (the "‑" in "Public‑Domain" is a
  non-breaking hyphen; copy it).
- **8 to 12 core concepts.** A concept name is a few words: either the
  texts' own phrase ("Resist the devil") or a plain label ("Hands laid
  on"). Never a claim ("X as fulfilment of Y", "Christ as ...").
- **B:** 3 to 8 passages per concept, each a reference the Berean has
  ("John 15:26", "Romans 8:14–17", "Galatians 4:6"), with, if you like, a
  short quotation in parentheses inside “curly quotes”, **copied exactly**
  from the verse. A note is a quotation or nothing: no comment.
- **C:** 4 to 10 secondary concepts, each "A → B (references)", the
  references in short form ("Rom 8:15; Gal 4:6").
- **D:** 4 to 10 links to other themes. Use **only these labels** for the
  other theme: Creation, Image of God, Blessing & Commission, Covenant,
  Kingdom, Law/Wisdom, Sacrifice, Priesthood, Temple/Presence,
  Sin/Exile/Death, Judgment, Mercy & Restoration, Deliverance/Salvation,
  Prophetic Word & Fulfillment, Messiah/Christology, Spirit, Church, New
  Creation, Wisdom Literature, Apocalyptic, and the four new ones: Father,
  Son and Holy Spirit; Spiritual Warfare; Freedom; Children of God. A link
  is kept only if the two themes cite a passage in common, so link where
  your passages truly overlap another theme's subject.

## The four themes, and what each must not do

- **THEME 22 — FATHER, SON AND HOLY SPIRIT** (id `trinity`). The passages
  that speak of the Father, the Son and the Spirit together; those that say
  God is one; and those that speak of them distinctly, side by side, in the
  texts' own words. **It says neither that the three are one God nor that
  they are not**; the word "Trinity" is the church's name for the teaching,
  not the text's, and appears nowhere in your concept names. **Include only
  passages that name at least two of the Father (or God), the Son (or
  Jesus, the Word) and the Spirit together, or that say God is one.** Leave
  out passages read as pointing to three persons without naming them --
  "Let Us make man" (Gen 1:26), "Holy, holy, holy" (Isa 6:3), the three
  visitors (Gen 18): those are readings. Jewish readers of the Hebrew Bible,
  and readers who do not hold the doctrine, must find every line true to
  the text.
- **THEME 23 — SPIRITUAL WARFARE** (id `spiritual_warfare`). **It hinges
  on Ephesians 6:10-18**, which is the first concept: "Put on the full
  armor of God, so that you can make your stand against the devil's
  schemes" (6:11), "our struggle is not against flesh and blood" (6:12),
  each piece of the armor in its own words. Then the texts'
  other words of struggle against evil powers: "Our struggle is not against flesh
  and blood", "the full armor of God", "Resist the devil", the accuser
  (Job 1-2, Zechariah 3, Revelation 12), unclean spirits cast out, Michael
  and the princes of Daniel 10, "the weapons of our warfare", "the prince of
  this world", "deliver us from the evil one". **Take no side** on whether
  "the satan" of Job and Zechariah is a title or a name, whether demons are
  cast out today and how, territorial spirits, or what the armor is. Report
  each verse's words.
- **THEME 24 — FREEDOM** (id `freedom`). The texts' words of bondage and
  release: "Do not be afraid", "Be anxious for nothing", "do not worry", "will not be put to
  shame", the yoke broken and the yoke offered (Matthew 11:29), "to set the
  oppressed free", "proclaim liberty", slaves to sin and "set free", "no
  longer a slave, but a son" (Romans 8:15, Galatians 4:7, John 8:35-36:
  these live here), "mastered" (1 Corinthians 6:12, 2 Peter 2:19 -- the
  texts' word; never "addiction"), the flesh and the Spirit, "It is for
  freedom that Christ has set us free". **Take no side** on freedom from the Law, free
  will, or freedom as political or inward.
- **THEME 25 — CHILDREN OF GOD** (id `children_of_god`). The texts' words of
  being God's children: "called children of God" (1 John 3:1, John 1:12),
  "sons of the living God" (Hosea 1:10), adoption to sonship, "Abba!
  Father!", heirs and co-heirs, "born of God", "I will be a Father to you",
  "Israel is My firstborn son" (Exodus 4:22), the peacemakers and those who
  love their enemies "called sons of God". **Leave to Freedom** the
  slave-to-son verses above. **Take no side** on who God's children are
  (all people, Israel, believers), on Israel and the church, or on whether
  being "born of water and the Spirit" (John 3:5) speaks of baptism -- quote
  it and stop.

Write the outline, and nothing else, as plain text to the path you are
given. Reply with one line: how many concepts, and any passage you could not
find in `bsb.txt`.
