# Reviewing a Sin, Judgment and Restoration table

A Bible website's theme pages -- meant to be the site's front doors -- each
lead to a table setting side by side what different books say of one thing.
Your packet is from one of four: **Sin, exile and death side by side**,
**Judgment side by side**, **Deliverance side by side**, or **Mercy and
restoration side by side**. Each row has the passages' own words in cells
(a cell marked "aside" uses the row's words of something else entirely, and
is shown with an asterisk), and two lines in the site's own voice:
**Alike** and **Different**. The site takes no position on matters
Christian traditions dispute, and adds no interpretation. The disputes
here:
- **Sin, Exile & Death:** how sin passes from the first man to others
  (inherited guilt, inherited corruption, ancestral sin); how far human
  nature is fallen; whether a verse's "death" is of the body, the soul or
  both; whether the exile has ended or ends for Israel in the future.
- **Judgment:** what hell is -- eternal conscious punishment; destruction
  (annihilation: the wicked destroyed, not kept in torment); or restoration
  in the end -- purgatory; the order and timing of the last
  things (a rapture, a millennium); whether judgment is by works, by faith,
  or both.
- **Deliverance:** how a person is saved (faith alone, faith and works, the
  sacraments); whether salvation can be lost; election and free will;
  whether all will be saved.
- **Mercy & Restoration:** whether all will be restored in the end; whether
  promises of restoration to Israel are to Israel, to the church, or to
  both, and whether they are yet to come.

For every row, check both lines -- and the title -- two ways.

1. **Against the texts.** Is every statement found in the cells' verses
   (the packet gives each verse in full)? Is every quotation word for word?
   Is any name, place or setting added that the cells' verses do not give?
   Nothing added: never what anything means, whether one passage looks
   forward to, fulfils or echoes another, or why it was written. **Watch the
   contrasts and the pairings most closely**: a row can take a side with
   every line in quotation, by setting two verses so that one answers the
   other ("eternal" beside "destroyed"; "by faith" beside "by works"), or so
   that one names who the other is about.

2. **As nine readers would take it.** Each judges within the 66 books all of
   them share, a constraint they agree to:
   - a **Lutheran** (justification by faith alone; law and gospel)
   - a **Reformed** Christian (election; the perseverance of the saints;
     total depravity)
   - a **Wesleyan** -- Methodist or Arminian (free will; that a believer can
     fall away)
   - a **Baptist / free church** Christian (eternal security; often
     dispensational: a future for Israel, a rapture)
   - a **Pentecostal / Charismatic** Christian
   - a **secular academic** scholar of the Bible (does the text say this?
     -- with Jewish readers of the Hebrew Bible in mind, for whom exile and
     return are their own history; and on hell, insists that destruction --
     annihilation -- be left open as one possible reading, beside eternal
     punishment and restoration: no line, title or pairing may close it off)
   - a **Catholic** (purgatory; faith and works; the sacraments)
   - an **Eastern Orthodox** Christian (ancestral sin, not inherited guilt;
     salvation as healing; some hope for all)
   - a **biblical-theological (literary)** reader (the Bible as one story;
     exile and return as its shape; the Old Testament heard on its own terms
     -- reading of the kind taught by BibleProject, Wheaton College Graduate
     School and Regent College, Vancouver)

   Would any of them object that a line, or the title, takes a side?

Verdicts, per row:
- `accept`: both lines and the title true to the texts, and all nine could
  accept them.
- `revise`: give the revised `alike` and/or `different` -- the smallest
  change that fixes it, quoting only the cells' verses -- and put any title
  change in `objections`. Name who objected and why.
- `withdraw`: the row cannot be stated neutrally.

Write a JSON array to the path you are given:

```json
[{"row": 3, "verdict": "accept", "alike": null, "different": null, "objections": []},
 {"row": 9, "verdict": "revise", "alike": null, "different": "...", "objections": ["Orthodox: ..."]}]
```

Reply with one line: how many accept / revise / withdraw.
