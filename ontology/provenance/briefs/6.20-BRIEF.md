# Reading the prophetic books for references to events

You are one of twenty-two readers. Your packet is part of Isaiah–Malachi
(Lamentations and Daniel included) in the Berean Standard Bible, every verse
numbered. Read **every verse** of the packet, in order. Find every place where
the prophet's words are tied to an **event**: a happening the Bible tells as
narrative (Genesis–Esther, the Gospels, Acts, and the narrative parts of other
books -- Daniel 1–6, Jonah, Isaiah 36–39, Jeremiah's narrative chapters).

## Three kinds, kept apart

Every reference you list is exactly one of these. Never merge them.

**`back` -- looking back.** The prophet names or retells an *earlier*
happening, with a detail from its account:
- "When Israel was a child I loved him, and out of Egypt I called My son"
  (Hosea 11:1) -- the exodus
- "remember what Balak king of Moab counseled and what Balaam son of Beor
  answered" (Micah 6:5) -- Numbers 22–24
- "Was it not You who cut Rahab to pieces ... who dried up the sea?"
  (Isaiah 51:9–10) -- the sea crossing
- "this is like the days of Noah to Me" (Isaiah 54:9) -- the flood

**`own_time` -- in their own time.** The text dates or places the prophecy
at a happening of its own day that a narrative tells, by its own words: a
king, a year, a named event.
- "In the year that King Uzziah died" (Isaiah 6:1) -- 2 Kings 15:7
- "Rezin king of Aram and Pekah son of Remaliah ... marched up to fight
  against Jerusalem" (Isaiah 7:1) -- 2 Kings 16:5
- "In the second year of King Darius ... the word of the LORD came through
  Haggai" (Haggai 1:1) -- Ezra 4:24–5:1
- "In the third year of the reign of Jehoiakim ... Nebuchadnezzar ... came to
  Jerusalem" (Daniel 1:1) -- 2 Kings 24:1
A passage the prophet's book tells *as narrative* that another book also
tells (Isaiah 36–39 and 2 Kings 18–20; Jeremiah 52 and 2 Kings 25) is
`own_time` with `how: "parallel"`. A date with no narrative telling of it
("two years before the earthquake", Amos 1:1) is listed with a note.

**`forward` -- looking forward.** A *later* passage that tells an event
**takes up this prophet's words** -- quotes them, or says the event happened
as the prophet said:
- Isaiah 7:14 and Matthew 1:22–23 (the birth of Jesus)
- Micah 5:2 and Matthew 2:5–6 (the magi at Herod's court)
- Zechariah 9:9 and Matthew 21:4–5 (the entry into Jerusalem)
- Joel 2:28–32 and Acts 2:16–21 (Pentecost)
- Jeremiah 25:11–12 and 2 Chronicles 36:21–22, Ezra 1:1 (the decree of Cyrus)
For `forward`, the later passage must quote or name the prophet's words.
A likeness of theme alone is **not** `forward`; do not list it.

## What does not count

- God's acts in general with no detail of an account ("He will judge the
  nations").
- Visions and oracles about the future that no later narrative takes up.
  (They are not events told; another part of the work maps them.)
- A narrative chapter of the prophet's own book telling an event that no
  other book tells (Jonah 1–4, Daniel 3) is that event's own telling, not a
  reference to it; list it only where it points at an account elsewhere.

When unsure, include it and say so in `note`; the judges who follow decide.
Missing a reference is worse than listing a doubtful one.

## Neutrality -- read this twice

Name what the text names. **Do not say what a prophecy means, whom it is
about, or when it is fulfilled**, beyond what the later text itself says.
For `forward`, record only that the later passage takes up the words and
what it says about them, in its own words. Where readers and traditions
dispute **which** happening a passage refers to, list it with
`"disputed": true` and every candidate passage -- do not choose.

## Output

Write one JSON file to the path you are given, exactly this shape:

```json
{
  "chapters_read": {"Isaiah 1": 0, "Isaiah 6": 1, "...": 0},
  "refs": [
    {
      "prophet": "Isaiah 6:1",
      "prophet_end": "Isaiah 6:1",
      "quote": "In the year that King Uzziah died",
      "kind": "own_time",
      "event": "Uzziah's death",
      "passages": ["2 Kings 15:7"],
      "later_quote": "",
      "listed_event": null,
      "how": "dated",
      "disputed": false,
      "note": ""
    }
  ]
}
```

- `chapters_read`: **every** chapter in your packet, with how many references
  you found in it (0 is a valid answer).
- `quote`: the prophet's own words, **copied exactly** from the packet (a
  machine checks every one). Shortest span that shows the reference.
- `kind`: `back`, `own_time` or `forward`, as above.
- `passages`: the narrative passage(s) that tell the event; for `forward`,
  the later passage that takes up the words.
- `later_quote`: for `forward` only, the later passage's words that take up
  the prophet's, as you recall them (a judge checks them); else "".
- `listed_event`: the matching title from `events.txt` if one fits, else null.
- `how`: `names`, `retells`, `dated`, `parallel`, `quoted` (the later text
  quotes the prophet), `fulfilled` (the later text says it happened as
  spoken), or `content` (only the content suggests the happening).
- One entry per reference.

When done, reply with one line: how many references of each kind, and how
many chapters.
