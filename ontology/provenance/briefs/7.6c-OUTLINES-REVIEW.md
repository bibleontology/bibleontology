# Reviewing a theme's redone outline

A Bible website maps the Bible's major themes. Below each theme's front page
is an **outline**: core concepts (the theme's sub-themes), the passages for
each, secondary concepts, and links to other themes. This outline has just
been redone from an earlier reader's draft, so that it speaks in the site's
voice: **every concept name and every note must be in the texts' own words or
a plain label -- never an interpretation**, and the site takes no side where
Christian traditions part. Your packet is one outline, with every passage
given in full in the Berean Standard Bible, and the drafter's own account of
what changed.

Two rulings of the site's owner (2026-10-08) that are not faults to report:
**overlap with the theme's table is welcome** ("a different way of
grouping"), and **there is no ceiling on the number of sub-themes** ("our
themes do not have to be balanced"). And one rule to check: **the last core
concept is the theme's own words of hope**, from passages within the theme,
with nothing dark left out or softened to make room.

For every core concept, check:

1. **The name against its passages.** Does every passage under it say what
   the name says? A name that says more than its passages, or that one
   passage says and the others do not, is renamed (in the texts' words) or
   the passage moved or dropped.
2. **The notes.** A note is an exact quotation of its verse or nothing.
3. **The passages.** Each holds the concept, not merely shares a word; none
   is longer than the concept needs.
4. **As nine readers would take it**: a Lutheran, a Reformed Christian, a
   Wesleyan, a Baptist / free church Christian (often dispensational), a
   Pentecostal / Charismatic, a secular academic scholar of the Bible (does
   the text say this? -- with Jewish readers of the Hebrew Bible in mind), a
   Catholic, an Eastern Orthodox Christian, and a biblical-theological
   (literary) reader. Would any object that a name, a grouping of passages,
   or the order of the concepts takes a side? -- above all on Old Testament
   passages set beside New Testament ones (no concept may say one is
   fulfilled in or points to the other), on the days of Genesis or how
   literally to read them, on what the image of God is, on Israel and the
   church, on the order of the last things.
5. **Completeness, as a reader of this theme would expect it**: is a
   sub-theme the texts plainly develop under this theme missing? Name it, with
   two or three references.

Write a JSON object to the path you are given:

```json
{"verdict": "accept" or "revise",
 "concepts": [{"n": 3, "verdict": "accept"},
              {"n": 5, "verdict": "revise", "name": "<new name or null>",
               "drop": ["<reference to drop>"], "note_fixes": ["<ref>: <fix>"],
               "why": "<reader: reason>"}],
 "secondary_or_links": ["<any change, with why>"],
 "missing": [{"sub_theme": "<name in the texts' words>", "refs": ["..."], "why": "..."}]}
```

Reply with one line: how many concepts accepted / revised, and how many
missing sub-themes you name.
