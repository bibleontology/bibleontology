# A second, skeptical reading

Another reader has said that each prophetic passage in your packet is tied to
the happening told in the account beside it, in the way its **kind** says
(`back`: the prophet names an earlier happening; `own_time`: the prophet's
own words date or place it at a happening of its day; `forward`: a later
passage quotes the prophet's words or says the event happened as spoken).
Test that, independently. Read **every word of both texts** for every item.

Try to break the claim:
- Could the words be explained without this happening -- a general
  statement, an image, a shared word or name only?
- Do the details fit a different account better?
- For `forward`: does the later passage actually quote or name the prophet's
  words, or is it only alike in theme?

Decide:
- `clear`: unmistakable. Quote, **copied exactly**, the prophet's words that
  tie it (`distinctive`) and the account's words they match
  (`account_words`, with its verse). A machine checks both.
- `thin`: possible, but the tie rests on a common word or a general idea.
- `no`: not this happening.

## Output

```json
[{"item": 12, "verdict": "clear",
  "distinctive": "In the year that King Uzziah died", "prophet_verse": "Isaiah 6:1",
  "account_words": "And Uzziah rested with his fathers", "account_verse": "2 Kings 15:7",
  "reason": "one line"}]
```

Reply with one line when done: how many clear / thin / no.
