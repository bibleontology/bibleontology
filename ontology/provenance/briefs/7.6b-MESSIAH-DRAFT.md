# The titles side by side: drafting the rows

A Bible website's "Messiah" theme page sets side by side the titles the
texts use -- Anointed, Messiah, Christ; Son of David; King of Israel; Son of
Man; Son of God; Servant; a prophet like Moses; a priest like Melchizedek;
Shepherd; Branch; Lamb; Stone; Immanuel; Holy One -- and where each is used,
from the Law to Revelation, **and of whom the text uses it**. One row per
title, in the texts' own words, with what is alike and what differs.

You are given `input.md`: for each title, every verse of the Berean Standard
Bible with its words, counted by column (the first 40 per column shown).
The whole Berean is in `bsb.txt`, one verse per line. **Look every quotation
up there and copy it exactly; never quote from memory.**

The table has these columns:
**The Law & history | Psalms & wisdom | The Prophets | Gospels & Acts | The
letters | Revelation**

## For each title, write one row

- `title`: the title, as the texts word it ("Son of Man", "the Lamb").
- `cells`: for each column where the title is used, **up to six** verses
  and the words that carry it, copied exactly, chosen to show the range of
  who the text uses it of and how: `{"The Prophets": [["Ezekiel 2:1", "Son
  of man,” He said to me"], ["Daniel 7:13", "One like the Son of Man coming
  with the clouds of heaven"]]}`. Where a column has many uses,
  include the ones where the text itself says who the title belongs to or
  what it is ("which is translated as Christ", John 1:41).
- `alike`: one or two sentences: what the uses share, quoting them.
- `different`: one or two sentences: **of whom** each text uses the title,
  as that text names them -- "Son of man" is how Ezekiel is addressed
  ("“Son of man,” He said to me", Ezekiel 2:1) and "One like the Son of Man"
  comes with the clouds in Daniel 7:13; "anointed" is said of Saul (1 Samuel 24:6), of David, and of Cyrus
  (Isaiah 45:1) -- and where the wording differs. You may give how many
  verses use the words in a column, **only** as the counts in `input.md`
  give them.

## The rule that governs every word

**No interpretation, of any kind.** Report who each text uses a title of,
as the text names them, and nothing more. Never say that a title used of
David, Solomon, Cyrus, Israel or Ezekiel "points to", "anticipates" or "is
fulfilled in" anyone; never call a passage "messianic"; never say who a
title "really" means, whether two uses refer to the same person, or what a
title says about anyone's nature. Where a later text applies a title to
Jesus, that is the text speaking: quote it. Readers and traditions --
Christian traditions, Jewish readers of the Hebrew Bible, secular scholars
-- read these titles very differently; every reader must be able to accept
every sentence, because every sentence only reports what the texts say.
"Isaiah 45:1 calls Cyrus the LORD's 'anointed'; John 1:41 has 'the Messiah
(which is translated as Christ)'" is right. "Cyrus prefigures the Messiah"
is wrong.

Every quotation is checked by machine against the verse you give.

## Output

Write a JSON array to the path you are given, one object per title, in the
order of `input.md`:

```json
[{"title": "Son of Man",
  "cells": {"The Prophets": [["Ezekiel 2:1", "stand up on your feet and I will speak to you"]],
            "Gospels & Acts": [["Mark 2:10", "the Son of Man has authority on earth to forgive sins"]]},
  "alike": "...", "different": "..."}]
```

Reply with one line: how many rows.
