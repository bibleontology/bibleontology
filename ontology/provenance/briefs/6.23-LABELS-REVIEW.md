# Reviewing the labels for links between the history books

A Bible website shows links from a passage in Genesis–Esther to an earlier
passage. Each link shows the two passages' own words side by side and one
fixed label saying what kind of link it is. The site takes no position on
matters Christian traditions dispute, and adds no interpretation.

The four kinds:
- **back**: the later passage names or retells an earlier happening.
- **fulfilled**: the later passage itself says something happened as an
  earlier word said ("according to the word that the LORD had spoken
  through Joshua", 1 Kings 16:34 / Joshua 6:26). It is shown only when the
  later text says so in its own words.
- **carried_out**: the later passage does what an earlier instruction
  commanded ("just as the LORD had commanded Moses").
- **quotes**: the later passage quotes the earlier one.

## The labels to review

On the later verse (the label is followed by the earlier passage's
reference):
1. back: "tells again what is told in"
2. fulfilled: "says it happened as was said in"
3. carried_out: "does as was commanded in"
4. quotes: "quotes"

On the earlier verse (the label is followed by the later passage's
reference):
5. back: "told again in"
6. fulfilled: "said to have happened as said here, in"
7. carried_out: "done as commanded here, in"
8. quotes: "quoted in"

Group labels (true by counting), for example:
9. "Exodus 36:1–39:43 carries out 27 instructions given in Exodus 25–31"
10. "Joshua 12:10–16 recalls 6 things told in Joshua 10"
11. "2 Kings 9:36–37 says 2 things happened as was said in 1 Kings 21"
12. "Nehemiah 8:14–15 quotes 2 passages of Leviticus 23"

Headings:
13. On the later verse: "Looks back"
14. On the earlier verse: "Looked back to later"

Note under the links:
15. "The later passage tells again, quotes or carries out the earlier one,
    or says in its own words that something happened as was said. The site
    shows what the texts say. It does not say what a prophecy or promise
    meant beyond that, or whether a later reading of it is right; readers
    and traditions differ on these."

## How to check

Check every label two ways:
1. **Against the definitions.** Does it say what the kind is, and no more?
   Above all: does "fulfilled" (2, 6, 11) ever sound like the site's own
   claim that a prophecy was fulfilled, rather than a report of what the
   later text says?
2. **As nine readers would take it.** Each judges within the 66 books all of
   them share: a **Lutheran**, a **Reformed** Christian, a **Wesleyan**, a
   **Baptist / free church** Christian (often dispensational), a
   **Pentecostal / Charismatic**, a **secular academic** scholar of the
   Bible, a **Catholic**, an **Eastern Orthodox** Christian, and a
   **biblical-theological (literary)** reader. Would any of them object that
   a label takes a side, or claims more than the text says?

Also: is each label plain English a newcomer understands?

## Result (2026-10-06)

10 accepted, 5 revised, all five applied: "tells again" became "recalls"
(1, 5, 15), because a `back` link may only name the earlier happening; 6
became "a later passage says this happened as said here, in" (the passive hid
who says it); 14 became "Looked back to by later passages".
