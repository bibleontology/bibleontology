# Judging Revelation's links to the rest of Scripture

Each item in your packet gives:
- a passage of Revelation, with one verse either side, the verses in
  question marked `>>`;
- another passage (elsewhere in Scripture, or in Revelation itself), the
  same way;
- the kind or kinds earlier readers proposed, and the words they matched.

All text is the Berean Standard Bible. Read **every word of both passages**
for every item. Do not judge from the readers' words.

## The question

Is there an **objective textual link** -- one any reader can see by setting
the two passages side by side, whatever they believe about Revelation? Decide
which kind it is (you may change the readers' kind):

- `words`: Revelation quotes or closely reproduces the other passage's
  **distinctive** wording ("Holy, holy, holy", Isa 6:3 / Rev 4:8; "every eye
  will see Him, even those who pierced Him", Zech 12:10 / Rev 1:7).
- `image`: both describe the same **distinctive** image or figure in shared
  terms (living creatures full of eyes with the faces of a lion, an ox, a man
  and an eagle; a beast with ten horns; a scroll eaten, sweet as honey). No
  claim that one points to the other.
- `names`: Revelation names a person, place, object or happening the other
  passage tells (Balaam and Balak; Jezebel; the song of Moses; the tree of
  life; manna).
- `key`: Revelation says in its own words what one of its images is ("the
  seven stars are the angels of the seven churches"), and the other passage
  is where that image appears.
- `shares_subject`: the same subject or a common word or title ("throne",
  "glory", "the Lord God Almighty", "the earth trembled"), with no
  distinctive detail in common. Not a link.
- `no`: not related as claimed.

Be strict. A word that occurs in many passages is not a link by itself; a
distinctive phrase, a rare combination, or an unmistakable image is. When
the readers proposed `words` but the passages share only an image, say
`image`.

## What you must give for `words`, `image`, `names` and `key`

`pairs`: one or more pairs of quotations, each **copied exactly**,
Revelation's words beside the other passage's words they correspond to:

```json
["every eye will see Him, even those who pierced Him", "Revelation 1:7",
 "they will look on Me, the One they have pierced", "Zechariah 12:10"]
```

A machine checks every quotation against the verse you give.

`why`: one sentence in plain words saying what the two passages have in
common, in their own terms ("Both describe four living creatures, full of
eyes, with the faces of a lion, an ox, a man and an eagle"). **No
interpretation**: never what an image stands for, whom it means, or when a
vision happens; never that one passage "fulfils" or "predicts" the other.
For `key`, say only what Revelation itself says the image is.

`disputed`: true if the other passage is one of several that Revelation's
words could be taking up and readers dispute which, with `other` naming the
rest. Otherwise false.

## Output

Write a JSON array to the path you are given, one object per item, in order:

```json
[{"item": 12, "verdict": "words", "pairs": [...], "why": "...",
  "disputed": false, "other": null, "reason": "one line, for the record"}]
```

Reply with one line when done: how many of each verdict.
