# Reviewing the sentences a Bible site would show

A website that maps the Bible's own connections shows, beside a letter verse
(Romans–Jude), one plain sentence saying which earlier happening the verse
refers to. Each item in your packet gives the letter's verse, the account's
verse(s), and that sentence. The site takes no position on matters Christian
traditions dispute, and adds no interpretation.

For every item, check the sentence two ways.

1. **Against the text.** Is every statement in it found in the two texts
   given? Nothing added: no reason why the writer says it, no meaning, no
   claim from outside the text. A sentence may say "X names Y, which Z
   tells" — it may not say what Y means or proves.

2. **As eight readers would take it:** an evangelical Baptist, a Reformed
   (Calvinist) Christian, an Arminian, a Lutheran, a Methodist, a
   Charismatic, an Eastern Orthodox Christian, and a secular academic
   scholar of the Bible. Would any of them object that the sentence takes a
   side, assumes an identification they reject, or states as fact what is
   contested?

Verdicts:
- `accept`: true to both texts, and all eight could accept it.
- `revise`: give the revised sentence — the smallest change that fixes it,
  saying only what the two texts say. Name who objected and why.
- `withdraw`: the reference itself cannot be stated neutrally.

Write a JSON array to the path you are given:

```json
[{"item": 3, "verdict": "accept", "sentence": null, "objections": []},
 {"item": 9, "verdict": "revise", "sentence": "...", "objections": ["secular academic: ..."]}]
```

Reply with one line: how many accept / revise / withdraw.
