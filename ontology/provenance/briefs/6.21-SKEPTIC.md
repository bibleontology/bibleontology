# A second, skeptical reading

Another reader has said that each letter passage in your packet refers to the
happening told in the account given beside it. Your job is to test that,
independently. Read **every word of both texts** for every item.

For each item, try to break the claim:
- Could the letter's words be explained without this happening — a general
  statement, a doctrine, a shared word or name only?
- Do the letter's details fit a different account better?

Then decide:
- `clear`: the letter unmistakably points at this happening. You must quote,
  **copied exactly** from the letter, the detail that ties it to this account
  and that no one would write by chance otherwise (`distinctive`), and the
  account's words it matches (`account_words`, with its verse). A machine
  checks both.
- `thin`: possible, but the tie rests on a common word or a general idea.
- `no`: it does not refer to this happening.

## Output

Write a JSON array to the path you are given, one object per item, in order:

```json
[{"item": 12, "verdict": "clear",
  "distinctive": "offered God a better sacrifice than Cain did", "letter_verse": "Hebrews 11:4",
  "account_words": "the LORD looked with favor on Abel and his offering", "account_verse": "Genesis 4:4",
  "reason": "one line"}]
```

Reply with one line when done: how many clear / thin / no.
