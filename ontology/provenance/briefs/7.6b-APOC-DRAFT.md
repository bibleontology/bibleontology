# The apocalyptic imagery table: drafting its rows

A Bible website's "Apocalyptic" theme page sets side by side how the books
describe the same images. Each item in your packet is one row: its title,
the links already judged between its passages, and every verse involved, in
full (Berean Standard Bible).

The table has these columns, by book:
**Daniel | Ezekiel | Isaiah | Jeremiah & the Twelve | Gospels & letters |
Elsewhere | Revelation** ("Jeremiah & the Twelve" is Jeremiah, Lamentations
and Hosea–Malachi; "Elsewhere" is any other book: Genesis, Exodus, Psalms...)

## For each row, write

- `cells`: for each column that has the image, the verses and **the words
  that carry the image**, copied exactly: `{"Ezekiel": [["Ezekiel 1:10",
  "the face of a lion on the right side"]], ...}`. Short spans. Leave out a
  column that does not have it.
- `alike`: one or two sentences saying what the passages share, in their
  own words, quoting them.
- `different`: one or two sentences saying where their wording differs --
  a detail one has and another lacks, a number, an order, who does what.
  Quote the words that differ. If nothing differs worth saying, `""`.

## The rule that governs every word

**No interpretation, of any kind.** Never say what an image stands for,
whom it means, whether one passage looks forward to, fulfils, echoes or
reinterprets another, why a writer changed something, or when anything
happens. Readers -- preterist, historicist, futurist, idealist, and many
others -- read these passages very differently; every one of them must be
able to accept every sentence, because every sentence only reports what
the texts say. "Ezekiel's creatures each had four faces; Revelation's are
each like one" is right. "Revelation simplifies Ezekiel's creatures" is
wrong (it says why). "The beast represents Rome" is wrong. Where a text
explains its own image ("the ten horns are ten kings"), quote it as the
text speaking.

Every quotation is checked by machine against the verse you give.

## Output

Write a JSON array, one object per row, in the packet's order:

```json
[{"row": 13, "cells": {"Ezekiel": [["Ezekiel 1:6", "each had four faces and four wings"]],
   "Revelation": [["Revelation 4:8", "each of the four living creatures had six wings"]]},
  "alike": "...", "different": "..."}]
```

Reply with one line: how many rows.
