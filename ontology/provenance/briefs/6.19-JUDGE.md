# Judging references from the poems to events

Each item in your packet gives:
- a passage from a poem (the Psalms, Job, Proverbs–Song of Solomon, or a
  poem inside a narrative book), with two verses either side for context, the
  verses in question marked `>>`. A psalm's title is printed at the start of
  its verse 1, as the Berean prints it;
- an event, by its title, and the narrative passage that tells it, in full;
- what an earlier reader said the reference is.

All text is the Berean Standard Bible. Read **every word of both texts** for
every item. Do not judge from the reader's note.

## The question

Does the poem **refer to this happening** — its title placing it there, or
the poem naming it, or retelling it with a concrete detail from this account
(who, where, when, what was done)?

- `refers`: yes. The poem, or its title, points at this happening.
- `suggests`: the poem's content fits this happening, but neither the title
  nor the poem names it, and other happenings could fit as well (a sanctuary
  burned, with no word of which one). Kept as a record, not shown.
- `same_subject`: the poem speaks of the same person, place or theme, but
  does not point at this happening (God's power over the sea in general, not
  the crossing; David as king, not this episode).
- `no`: not this happening (the reader matched the wrong account, or nothing
  is referred to).

Be strict. A shared word is not a reference. A shared person is not a
reference. A title naming only an author is not a reference. A detail that
**belongs to this account** is.

## What you must give for `refers` and `suggests`

`pairs`: one or more pairs of quotations, each **copied exactly**, the poem's
words beside the account's words that they correspond to:

```json
["when he fled from his son Absalom", "Psalm 3:1",
 "Arise and let us flee, or we will not escape from Absalom!", "2 Samuel 15:14"]
```

A machine checks every quotation against the verse you give. Shortest spans
that show the correspondence.

`why`: one sentence, in plain words, saying what the poem refers to, in the
text's own terms. **No interpretation**: not what it means, not why the
poet says it, not who wrote it beyond what the title says, nothing a
tradition would dispute. Example: "The psalm's title places it when David
fled from Absalom, which 2 Samuel 15 tells."

`disputed`: true if the poem's details also fit another account and readers
dispute which is meant, with `other` naming it. Otherwise false.

## Output

Write a JSON array to the path you are given, one object per item, in the
packet's order:

```json
[{"item": 12, "verdict": "refers", "pairs": [...], "why": "...",
  "disputed": false, "other": null, "reason": "one line, for the record"}]
```

`reason` is for the record, not shown: why you decided as you did, in one
line. Reply with one line when done: how many refers / suggests /
same_subject / no.
