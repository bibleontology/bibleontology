# Reviewing the apocalyptic imagery table

A Bible website's "Apocalyptic" theme page -- meant to be one of the site's
front doors -- has a table setting side by side how Daniel, Ezekiel, Isaiah,
the other prophets, the Gospels and letters, and Revelation describe the
same images. Each row has the passages' own words in cells, and two lines
in the site's own voice: **Alike** and **Different**. The site takes no
position on matters Christian traditions dispute, and adds no
interpretation. Revelation and Daniel are where traditions differ most.

For every row, check both lines two ways.

1. **Against the texts.** Is every statement found in the cells' verses?
   Nothing added: never what an image stands for, whom it means, when it
   happens, that one passage fulfils, predicts, echoes or reinterprets
   another, or why a writer wrote as he did. **Watch the contrasts most
   closely**: a "Different" line can carry an interpretation without one
   interpretive word, by what it chooses to set side by side -- pairing
   Daniel's "Ancient of Days" with Revelation's "One like the Son of Man" as
   if to make a point about who is who. A contrast is fair only if it
   reports what each text says and draws nothing from the pairing.

2. **As nine readers would take it.** Each judges within the 66 books all of
   them share, a constraint they agree to:
   - a **Lutheran** (justification by faith alone; law and gospel; the sacraments)
   - a **Reformed** Christian (election; the perseverance of the saints)
   - a **Wesleyan** -- Methodist or Arminian (free will; that a believer can fall away)
   - a **Baptist / free church** Christian (believer's baptism; often dispensational)
   - a **Pentecostal / Charismatic** Christian (the gifts of the Spirit)
   - a **secular academic** scholar of the Bible (reads under no confession: does the text say this?)
   - a **Catholic** (Scripture read with the Church's tradition)
   - an **Eastern Orthodox** Christian (Scripture read with the Church's tradition)
   - a **biblical-theological (literary)** reader (the Bible as one story; its
     literary design; the Old Testament heard on its own terms before it is
     read as pointing forward -- reading of the kind taught by BibleProject,
     Wheaton College Graduate School and Regent College, Vancouver)

   Would any of them -- preterist, historicist, futurist or idealist; on
   the millennium, the rapture, the tribulation, Israel and the church --
   object that a line takes a side or assumes an identification?

Verdicts, per row:
- `accept`: both lines true to the texts, and all nine could accept them.
- `revise`: give the revised `alike` and/or `different` -- the smallest
  change that fixes it, quoting only the cells' verses. Name who objected
  and why.
- `withdraw`: the row cannot be stated neutrally.

Write a JSON array to the path you are given:

```json
[{"row": 3, "verdict": "accept", "alike": null, "different": null, "objections": []},
 {"row": 9, "verdict": "revise", "alike": null, "different": "...", "objections": ["Futurist Baptist: ..."]}]
```

Reply with one line: how many accept / revise / withdraw.
