## For the review panel (contested points, by row)

1. **Rows 4, 5, 6 (the persons).** Matt 16:27, Mark 8:38, Luke 9:26, Phil 2:11, 2 Pet 1:17, John 8:54 and John 17:5, 17:24 are in both the Father and the Christ rows, as the verse names both. The Lamb's doxologies are now in the Christ row (Rev 5:12-13), also in row 12 (Rev 5:13 addresses God and the Lamb). Trinitarian readers: expected. Readers who do not read the Son as a recipient of glory may take the listing as a claim; each row's note says the page does not choose how the text relates them.
2. **Rows 10 and 11, Isaiah on its own.** The "I will not yield My glory to another" row is now Hebrew only; John 17:22 ("I have given them the glory You gave Me") is in row 11 by rule and also, as Christ and Father are named, in rows 4 and 5 (John 17:5, 17:24). The panel's rabbi and Jewish seat should read row 10 alone. Muslim, Unitarian and Trinitarian readers may each want a view on whether the two rows being apart is itself a reading.
3. **Rows 3 and 13 ("the Lord", "Him"):** 2 Cor 3:18 ("the Lord, who is the Spirit") is in rows 3 and 6; Heb 13:21 and 1 Pet 4:11 ("through Jesus Christ, to whom") are in row 13: Catholic, Orthodox and Protestant readers part on the antecedent. The note says the page does not choose.
4. **Verb "Jesus glorified", John 16:14** ("He will glorify Me"): the verse says "He", the sentence before names the Spirit of truth; note kept.
5. **Row 17 (called to glory ...), Rom 9:23 and verb Rom 8:30:** election and predestination; Reformed, Arminian, Catholic and Orthodox readers part. Note: the page takes no side. The label is only the text's verbs and nouns.
6. **2 Cor 3:7-11 (now in row 20):** a Jewish reader may hear supersession in "fading glory"; the note on row 20 says the page does not say what the side-by-side implies for the Law or Israel. It is no longer a labelled row: if the panel thinks the note too easy to miss, it can go back to a row.
7. **Row 14, 1 Cor 11:7 and 11:15:** "the woman is the glory of man"; complementarian and egalitarian readers part. Plain listing with a no-side note, now inside a long row and beside John 12:43 and Rom 2:7.
8. **Rows 7 and 8 (appears, fills):** the Orthodox hear glory as uncreated light, the Jewish reader as the kavod that dwelt in the Temple. The rows say what the verses say (appears, fills, departs, returns); Ezek 9-11 and 43-44 are in row 8 with no reading of whether the glory is God or God's presence. They are now bigger rows (60 and 45).
9. **Row 15 (exchanged):** the Berean capitalises "Glory" at Ps 106:20, Jer 2:11, Hos 4:7; the label does not say whose. Rom 1:23 is explicit.
10. **Row 19 (the merged other-sense row):** two different facts in one row, with a note naming both. A panel member may prefer the two kinds apart; the 40-row spec has them apart. The Ps 16:9 point stands: MACULA glosses Ps 16:9 'glory' but Ps 30:12, 108:1 and 57:8 'soul', though the phrase is the same.
11. **Row 12:** doxologies and purpose clauses are one row ("Glory to God; to the glory of God; to the praise of His glory"). Rev 5:13 is both there and in the Christ row.

## What the builder could not express (unchanged, plus one)

- A `pick` verse outside the sense rule shows no word in its example (row 19 has 46 such verses). Option needed: attach the concept's word to a picked verse whatever its gloss.
- No "genitive position" test (the word after "of"), so the people row is picked by hand.
- No all-of test inside `in_verse`: a row cannot require two companion words at once.
- No per-word `sense` regex.
- New with the consolidation: a row cannot be both rule and pick (`pick` skips the rule entirely), so merging a phrase-rule row with a hand-picked row means making the whole row picks, or using `add` for the extras, as the LORD row does for Ps 3:3 and Zech 2:5. An option letting `pick` extend a rule row would keep the rule's coverage.
