## Contested points for the review panel (row, verses, who is concerned)

1. **"The Holy Spirit" (89 verses; the heaviest single row).** Pure title, noted as such; the 4 noun verses ("the spirit of Your holiness", Ps 51:11, Isa 63:10-11; "the Spirit of holiness", Rom 1:4) are a separate noun row. Concerned: secular/Jewish readers (Spirit of holiness as God's own holiness at work, not a title) and Trinitarian readers (the row presumes nothing about the person). Eph 1:13 is picked in though the adjective stands after "of promise". **Rom 15:19 is left out**: the Greek has ἅγιος (the original arbitrates), the Berean prints "the Spirit of God"; a row labelled "The Holy Spirit" would show a verse without the words. It stands in "Every other use". Also not in the hub though the Berean prints "Holy Spirit": Acts 8:16 (the Greek has no ἅγιος there).
2. **"Jesus called holy" (7).** By pick: Acts 4:27, 4:30 (names Jesus), Mark 1:24 and Luke 4:34 (said by an unclean spirit: "Jesus of Nazareth ... the Holy One of God"), John 6:69 (Peter, to Jesus), Acts 3:14, Luke 1:35 (the child is named in 1:31, not in the verse). Concerned: Jewish/secular (who is "the Holy One of God"), and any tradition that hears a demon's word as testimony.
3. **"The Holy One", "holy and true": the title without a name (9).** Job 6:10, Prov 9:10, 30:3, Isa 10:17, 40:25, Hos 11:12, **1 John 2:20, Rev 3:7, Rev 6:10**. The note says OT readers take it of the LORD, and for the last three some take the Father, some Christ; the page does not choose. Concerned: Reformed, Catholic, Orthodox (Christ), Jewish/secular (YHWH).
4. **"The LORD, the Holy One of Israel" (37).** Includes Isa 49:7 (the Servant is addressed in the same verse) and Hos 11:9, Hab 1:12, 3:3, each with the LORD or God named in the verse. Isa 49:7: the label is the title only.
5. **Holy angels, "holy ones", "the saints of the Most High" (22).** Dan 7:18, 21, 22, 25, 27 (angels or the LORD's people: Jewish, Catholic, Reformed, dispensational readers differ), 1 Thess 3:13, 2 Thess 1:10, Jude 14 (angels or believers). The note says the page does not choose. Ps 16:3 and 34:9 ("saints") are put under "A holy people" because the verse sets them in the land / among those who fear the LORD; Deut 33:3 and Zech 14:5 are under "holy ones".
6. **How the saints come to be holy: Wesleyan, Pentecostal, Reformed, Catholic, Orthodox.** Laid side by side, never ranked:
   - adjective "Called to be saints, called and chosen to be holy" (Rom 1:7, 1 Cor 1:2, Eph 1:4, 5:27, Col 1:22, 3:12, 2 Tim 1:9, Heb 3:1), with a note;
   - noun "Holiness that is God's" (Heb 12:10, 2 Cor 1:12, 1 Cor 1:30, 2 Thess 2:13, 1 Pet 1:2) beside "Holiness as the aim and fruit of the life" (Heb 12:14, 2 Cor 7:1, Rom 6:19, 6:22, 1 Thess 3:13, 4:3, 4:4, 4:7, 1 Tim 2:15);
   - verb "Having been sanctified", "you were sanctified" (1 Cor 1:2, 6:11, Acts 20:32, 26:18, Rom 15:16, Heb 10:10, 10:29, 2 Tim 2:21) beside "Being sanctified", "sanctify you completely", "let the holy continue to be holy" (Heb 10:14, 1 Thess 5:23, Rev 22:11, John 17:17, 17:19).
   The panel should check one thing in particular: **the grouping itself is a reading** (the Greek aorist and perfect passives under "done", the present participle and wishes under "ongoing or asked"). The labels are the Berean's English only, the notes say the page takes no side and a verse that fits both stands in both (John 17:17, 17:19 and 1 Cor 1:2 appear twice or more), but a Wesleyan will hear 1 Thess 5:23 "completely" in the second row and a Reformed reader "you were sanctified" in the first as settling it. If the panel finds the grouping tips, the fallback is one row of all 13+5 verses labelled only "Sanctified, sanctify".
7. **"Sanctified by: the truth, the word, faith, the blood, the sacrifice, the Spirit, a believing spouse" (13).** Lists the means each verse names, unranked. 1 Cor 7:14 is in it because the verse says "sanctified" and names a means; it is also a verse in the baptism debates (Catholic, Orthodox, Reformed, Baptist). The page says nothing of the children. Heb 9:13 (blood of goats and bulls) is the same verb for the old rites.
8. **"Jesus sanctifies" (4) and "God sanctifies" (22).** By pick. "God sanctifies" includes Num 3:13, 8:17, 1 Kgs 9:3, 9:7, 2 Chr 7:16, 7:20 (the LORD's own first-person words, the speaker named in the speech), John 10:36 ("the Father sanctified"), 1 Thess 5:23 ("the God of peace Himself"). "Jesus sanctifies" includes Eph 5:26, which does not name Christ in the verse (5:25 does).
9. **"Hallow the name ... 'sanctify Christ as Lord'" (19).** 1 Pet 3:15 puts Christ where Isa 8:13 puts the LORD of Hosts; the label keeps both phrases as written and equates neither.
10. **"The LORD our God is holy", "Holy Father", "Holy is His name" (14).** Rev 4:8 "Lord God Almighty", John 17:11 "Holy Father", Luke 1:49 "the Mighty One ... Holy is His name". Rev 3:7 and 6:10 ("holy and true") are deliberately not here but in row 3.
11. **"Written to the saints" and "The saints in Acts and Revelation".** "Saints" kept as the Berean writes it. A Catholic or Orthodox reader hears the word differently from a Protestant; the rows say what each verse says (written to, served, harmed) and no more.
12. **"Prepare for battle", "prepare for war", "declare war" (5).** Jer 6:4, 51:27, 51:28, Joel 3:9, Mic 3:5: the Hebrew verb is קָדַשׁ (literally to sanctify a war). The Berean does not print "consecrate" in them; the note says so. Not a doctrine point, a "true to the text" one.
13. **Hebrew qodesh "holy mountain" under the noun, Greek "holy city" under the adjective.** The sections follow the part of speech; the hub's `_about` says so. A reader may think they are different things; they are not (the LXX renders one by the other).

## What the builder could not express (not edited; options wanted)

1. **Row order: fixed by the coordinator** (rows now list in the Bible's book order). `others.add`, `sense_drop` and the catch-all's `not_verses` are also in the builder now; `others.add` is used for חָסִיד.
2. **Nine verses of the family are absent from the lexicon's occurrence list** (`S.occurrences`): Lev 10:4, 16:17, 1 Chr 16:35, Ps 106:47, Ezek 36:38, 42:20, 44:7, 44:23, 2 Chr 15:18. A rule can never place them, `add` needs the verse "seen" and cannot, and a row cannot hold both `pick` and a rule. So the rows "The sanctuary", "My holy name" and "Holy things" became explicit lists (first found by the rule, then added to). Wanted: `add` (or `pick`) that works beside a rule, and a "missing from the occurrence list" check in the build output.
3. **Versification slips in the lexicon.** The Hebrew's 1 Sam 21:5-7 are printed as 21:5-7 where the Berean has them at 21:4-6 (so Doeg's verse showed as a holy-bread verse and the "consecrated bread" verse was missing), and the Hebrew's Hos 12:1 for the Berean's 11:12. Fixed by `moves`, one entry each. Wanted: the build should flag a placed verse whose English lacks every gloss the lexicon gives the word. (My script check found only these two; there may be quieter ones where the neighbour verse also says "holy".)
4. **`others` reads MACULA's gloss, not the Berean's English** (חָסִיד "saints"); worked round with `others.add`.
5. **A fallback ("no condition") row takes only verses the builder "saw"** in its pass over the clauses and frames. Isa 66:17 (a verb with no frame) could not be caught and had to be picked into the "Consecrate yourselves" row (its text says "consecrate ... themselves").
6. **No rule on the English rendering.** The Berean's "Holy Place", "sacred donations", "dedicated gifts", "holy ground" for the same קֹדֶשׁ are what a reader sees; the rows by that rendering are picks I generated from the Berean's glosses. A `gloss` condition on the word's rendering would have made these rule rows.
7. **Frames are unreliable as agents for these verbs** (Moses/people/ "הוּא" as agent; many have no agent), so every verb row about who sanctifies is a pick, not an `agent` rule.
8. **No way to put a word in a section other than its part of speech.** `forms` would have moved קֹדֶשׁ under "adjective" so Hebrew and Greek "holy city" met, but it also relabels the word as "Hebrew adjective" in "The words the text uses", which is false. A per-row `section` field would do it.
