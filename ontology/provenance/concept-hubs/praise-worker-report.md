**Others** ("Other words translated praise"): יָדָה (24 verses where the Berean prints "praise"), רֹומַם (2), נָוָה (Exod 15:2), נִיב (Isa 57:19), הִלּוּלִים (Lev 19:24), שָׂגָא (Job 36:24). Excluded by hand with reason: ἀνθομολογέομαι (Luke 2:38, the Berean prints "gave thanks") and the Aramaic ידה (Dan 6:10, "gave thanks").

**The word-rule decisions** (the ones the owner asked me to make):
- **יָדָה / תּוֹדָה are `others`, not rows.** Reasons: the card itself gives יָדָה the gloss "to give thanks"; in the Berean it is "give thanks" 50, "praise" 22, "confess" 13; the committee asked praise and thanks kept apart; and it is the φιλέω / חֶסֶד case (a different word the English also renders so). Only the verses where the Berean says "praise" appear, under Other words. The row "Praise and thanks, side by side" sets the two together as the text does, without equating them.
- **ἐξομολογέω** (the Greek of ידה in Rom 15:9, "I praise You, Father" Matt 11:25) likewise apart: see "could not express".
- **δοξάζω is in the rows**, because the Greek puts it beside αἰνέω (Luke 2:20 "glorifying and praising God"). It overlaps the glory hub: three rows say so (the Son/Father row note, the "Glorified ..." row note, the "Glorify God" row doors to glory). The Father/Son verses are one pick row (rule 4).
- **εὐλογέω / בָּרַךְ ("bless") not in the rows**, per the brief. The Berean prints "bless", never "praise", for them, so none reach Other words either. "Bless the LORD, O my soul" (Ps 103:1, 104:1, 134:1, 145:1-2), "Blessed be the LORD" and Luke 1:64 are therefore absent from the hub (see below).

## Contested points for the review panel

1. **Praise versus thanks (יָדָה, תּוֹדָה).** The hub does not equate them. Readers who hear "I will give thanks to the LORD" as praise (Ps 7:17, 9:1, 138:1 are יָדָה, mostly "thank" in the Berean) will not find those verses in the rows; they are named once under Other words, and only where the Berean prints "praise". Decision for the owner. Pentecostal / free-church readers most likely to notice.
2. **"Bless the LORD".** Not reachable (below). A reader of the Psalms will miss Ps 103, 104, 134, 145 ("Bless the LORD, O my soul"). The Berean's own English separates it; the panel should say whether the hub should carry it as a row of its own with a note.
3. **"Boast in the LORD" (הִתְהַלֵּל).** Ps 34:2, 44:8, Jer 9:24, 1 Chr 16:10, Ps 105:3, 106:5 are the same Hebrew verb, printed "boast / glory in", left out as another sense. Jewish and academic readers may want them.
4. **The Son glorified, the Father glorified in the Son** (John 7:39, 8:54, 11:4, 12:16, 12:23, 12:28, 13:31-32, 14:13, 15:8, 16:14, 17:1-10; Acts 3:13; Heb 5:5; Luke 4:15). All by hand, with a note that the page takes no side and that the Greek is one verb, δοξάζω, used for "they glorified God". Catholic, Orthodox, Reformed, Anglican, Baptist readers will read the persons differently (John 16:14 is "He", the Spirit glorifying Jesus; John 17:5 glory "before the world existed"). The label is the text's own. Rows where Christ receives praise: there is none; no verse here has anyone praising Jesus, only glorifying him in John and Luke 4:15 ("glorified by everyone").
5. **Rom 8:30** ("those He justified, He also glorified") and **Acts 13:48** ("glorified the word of the Lord, and all who were appointed for eternal life believed") sit in the "Glorified ..." row only because the Greek verb is δοξάζω. Both verses carry election / ordo salutis readings; the label quotes the object glorified and nothing else. The panel should confirm they do not look like the hub taking a side by being listed. Acts 13:48 is also in the nations row.
6. **Rom 1:21** ("neither glorified Him as God nor gave thanks") is in "Who does not praise or glorify" beside the dead (Ps 115:17, Isa 38:18). Readers who take Rom 1 as natural revelation versus fallenness read the verse differently; the label asserts only what it says.
7. **Worship style.** No row groups by instruments, dance, raised hands or liturgy. Ps 149:3 and 150:3-5 appear only as verses of the rows by who is called / the call itself. The Levites row is by who the text names (Chronicles), not by form of worship; Catholic / Orthodox readers might read it as liturgy, Baptist readers as old covenant.
8. **Praise given to a person or to gods** (Prov 31, Absalom, Song 6:9, Dan 5:23, Judg 16:24, 1 Cor 11). The row is the text's own: the praise of a wife is not hidden, the praise of "their gods" is not equated with the LORD's. Eccl 4:2 ("admired the dead") and 2 Chr 23:12 ("cheering the king") are the Berean's rendering of שׁבח / הלל; shown there by hand.
9. **Hallelujah.** The note says יָהּ is a short form of the LORD's name (standard; no tradition disputes it, but it is a reading of the form).
10. **Heb 2:12 and Matt 21:16** (Jesus quoted at Ps 22:22 / Ps 8:2) are in the first-person and "every other use" rows respectively; neither is a claim that praise is offered to Christ.

## What the builder could not express

- **`others` cannot take a verse by hand or look at the Berean text.** It matches MACULA's in-verse gloss. MACULA glosses Matt 11:25 and Luke 10:21 (ἐξομολογέω, the Berean's "I praise You, Father") as "I fully consent", so these two key verses are in no row and not in Other words. Wanted: `others.add` (key -> verses) or a text-based match.
- **`others` cannot condition on the object.** "Bless the LORD" (בָּרַךְ / εὐλογέω with God as the one blessed) is wanted as a different word apart; the gloss rule would take every blessing of people too. Wanted: an `others` entry with a patient or an `in_verse`.
- **No way to drop a word's verses by hand.** The δοξάζω uses that are not praise (Rom 8:30, 2 Cor 3:10, Rev 18:7, 2 Thess 3:1) cannot be struck from the words; I gave them an honest labelled row instead. Wanted: `sense_drop` mirroring `sense_keep`.
- **Hebrew frames are noisy for "who" and "whom".** Patient הוּא ('Him') is unresolved, so about forty "Praise Him" verses cannot be put under the LORD by rule; agents can come from a neighbouring verse. Worked round with `agent` AND `in_verse` and by hand.
- **A verb row cannot condition on which concept word the verse carries** (sing vs praise vs glorify), so "sing praises (זמר)" is not its own row; its verses are placed by who sings and whom.
- ἁλληλουϊά has no frames, so it only appears as a noun row.
