## The greeting and "welfare" sense (Doug's note)

`sense` is not "peace" alone. It is `peace|welfare|\bwell\b|all right|safe|secur|prosper|health|\bfare\b|greet|\bkind\b|favorable|trusted|friend|unscathed|wholly|triumph|how ~ was|harmony`, which are the glosses MACULA gives these words. Effect: all 208 verses of שָׁלוֹם count (0 dropped), where "peace" alone would have kept about 150 and hidden the greeting, the question "Is it well?", safety and prosperity. The rows then set the senses apart in the text's own words: "Peace to you", "Go in peace", "Do you come in peace?", "Asked after, greeted: Is it well?" (the Berean prints "well", "all right", "welfare", "how they were doing", "greet" for the same Hebrew word), "Safe, in safety, returned safely", "Welfare, well-being, prosperity", "The friends, the allies, the man of my peace". Their notes say the Hebrew word is the same. Rule-based rows could not do this (no companion word marks the idiom), so these are hand picks, each from the glosses and the Berean text, with `_why`.

## Contested points for the review panel

1. **Rom 5:1 "peace with God ... justified through faith"** (rows: "Peace with God", "Peace in Christ", "Peace and faith"). Reformed/Lutheran take it as a verdict; Catholic/Orthodox and others as a relation restored. Note says the page takes no side. Also Isa 27:5 and Job 22:21 ("be at peace with Him") are set beside it as the text's other "peace with God" phrases.
2. **Isa 9:6-7 "Prince of Peace"** (own row). Jewish readers take it of Hezekiah, Christians of Christ. Note says so; no door to Jesus.
3. **"He" row (Mic 5:5, Eph 2:14, Eph 2:15, Ps 147:14, Zech 9:10)**. The text says "He"; note says readers take it of the LORD, the Messiah or Christ. Mic 5:5 and Zech 9:10 are read of the Messiah by many Christian and some Jewish readers; Eph 2:14 follows a verse that names Christ. The door lists include jesus and theme_messiah and god together; the panel should confirm that doors alone do not choose.
4. **Gal 6:16 "the Israel of God"** (row "Peace upon Israel"). Supersessionist, dispensational and Jewish readers differ; Baptist/free church (dispensational) flagged for this concept. Note takes no side. Gal 6:16 is also in the "Grace, mercy and peace" wish row.
5. **Eph 2:15 "abolishing in His flesh the law of commandments and decrees ... thus making peace"** (law row; also the "He" row). Note: readers part on what is abolished; page takes no side. The row says only that the verse stands beside the law.
6. **Luke 2:14 vs Matt 10:34 / Luke 12:51** (row "Peace on the earth, peace in heaven"). Set side by side as written; the Rev 6:4 "take peace from the earth" stands with them. Note says no side taken on how they fit.
7. **John 14:27, 16:33** (own row: "Peace I leave with you"). The verse does not name the speaker; note says "the Gospel of John". The panel should decide whether the door to Jesus is allowed there or should be removed.
8. **Col 1:20 "making peace through the blood of His cross"** and **Job 22:21** (reconciliation). Atonement theories (penal substitution, Christus Victor, theosis) all read it; note takes no side; the verse is placed alone in the verb form.
9. **Divine-person rows are by hand**: "Peace be with you, said by Jesus" (4 verses naming Him), "Peace from the LORD, from God" (only verses naming the LORD or God), "Peace in Christ" (verses naming Christ or Jesus). "The peace of God, the God of peace" is the one rule row about God (`with @divine, adjacent`), as in righteousness and love, with a note. Panel should confirm it is acceptable.
10. **Peace and the Holy Spirit** is deliberately not a row: a rule on πνεῦμα would be a row about a divine person; Gal 5:22 stands as "the fruit of the Spirit", and Rom 14:17, 15:13 appear only in "Peace and joy" / "righteousness".
11. **"Peace and being made holy"** (Heb 12:14, 1 Thess 5:23, 1 Pet 1:2): holiness sensitivity (sanctification, Wesleyan / Reformed). Label only says the words stand together.
12. **"Peace and faith"** row: the committee flagged faith high; verses are listed as written (Luke 7:50, Mark 5:34, Rom 5:1, 15:13, Heb 11:31), no claim about what faith does.
13. **The Berean's rendering of ambiguous Hebrew** in hand-picked rows: "in triumph" (Judg 8:9, 11:31), "wholly" (Jer 13:19), "a sound answer" (Gen 41:16) are the Hebrew שָׁלוֹם and remain in "Peace in other uses". Original and translation differ in these.
14. **Lead/`doors` choices**: war, covenant, jerusalem, peace_offering etc. in `leads`; none are interpretive. "Jerusalem" door is in the row "Pray for the peace of Jerusalem" (Jer 29:7 is about Babylon; the row label covers it as "the city").

## What the builder could not express

- **Versification**: `in_verse` reads the original's verse numbering for some chapters. For 1 Chr 12:18 the words counted ("adversaries") belong to the original's neighbouring verse, so the war row took a verse whose Berean text has no war. Needed: `in_verse` read from the same verse mapping as the occurrences. Worked around with `not_verses`.
- **A pick row cannot also carry a rule**. Rows with `pick` skip all rule keys, so "Peace on the earth" needed `add` for Luke 19:38 instead of a mixed row; fine here, but a "pick + rule" row would have been simpler.
- **Row conditions on the English gloss**. The greeting/welfare sense is visible only in MACULA's gloss ("welfare", "well", "all right", "greet") and the Berean text; `sense` can filter a word's verses by gloss but a row cannot be defined by it. Needed: a row key `gloss` (regex on the verse's gloss for the word) would have made "Asked after, greeted", "Safe" and "Welfare" rule-built instead of hand-picked.
- **Verb form from the lexicon**: h7999 שָׁלַם is filed as a noun (and g1518 εἰρηνοποιός as an adjective), so its verb frames were never used; its 12 verses are placed by hand in noun rows ("Make peace with", "Be at peace with").
- **`sense` is global**, one regex for all words. A per-word sense (שָׁלַם only "make peace / at peace") would have been exact; the global regex happens to work because no repay-gloss matches.
- **Pick verses with empty Berean text**: 3 John 1:15 is printed in 1:14 (handled with `moves`).
- **`others` takes only the gloss "peace"**: שַׁלְוָה's other glosses (ease, security, prosperity, quiet; Ps 122:7, Prov 17:1 etc.) and the neighbours בֶּטַח and שָׁקַט are not shown. A per-word gloss list on `others` would show them.
