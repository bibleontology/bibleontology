## What was tried and dropped

- Rule rows on the frames' agent and patient for "The LORD saves ...": 12 of 100-odd Hebrew verses reached them (the rest have no agent in the frames). Replaced by hand (rule 4 asks for that anyway).
- Rule rows "Saved from enemies" / "Saved from sin, wrath and death" by `in_verse`: the Hebrew lemma for "enemy" did not match, and "enemies" caught Rom 5:10 ("when we were enemies of God") and צַר "distress" (Ps 107). "Enemies" is the whole of row 1; "from" is now a short row by hand.
- A row "Saved, and the name": dropped for count (Acts 4:12 and Rom 10:13 are in row 6, Matt 1:21 in row 5 and row 10).
- A rule row for rock/light/strength: took Isa 49:6 ("a light for the nations") and 2 Chr 6:41 ("the ark of Your might"); now by hand.

## For the review panel (contested points, specific)

1. **Whether salvation can be lost** (rows 17 and 18, verb form). Row 17: Eph 2:5, 2:8, 2 Tim 1:9, Titus 3:5, Rom 8:24, Luke 7:50 ("has saved") and 1 Cor 1:18, 2 Cor 2:15, Acts 2:47, 1 Cor 15:2 ("are being saved"). Row 18: "will be saved" (Rom 10:9, 10:13, 11:26, Mark 16:16, Acts 2:21), "to the end" (Matt 10:22, 24:13, Mark 13:13), "if you hold firmly" (1 Cor 15:2), "if they continue" (1 Tim 2:15), 1 Cor 3:15, 1 Cor 5:5, 1 Tim 4:16, Jer 23:6, 30:7, 33:16. Reformed/Baptist (security) vs Arminian/Wesleyan/Catholic/Orthodox (perseverance) readers should check: is the split by the verb's tense an even hand, and is it fair that 1 Cor 15:2 stands in both rows? The note names John 10:28 ("they will never perish") and Heb 6:6 ("and then have fallen away") as not in the hub (no word for "saved"), one for each side: is that balanced or a thumb on the scale? Jude 5 ("after Jesus had delivered His people out of the land of Egypt, He destroyed those who did not believe") is in the Jesus row, not in either of these: should it stand in row 18? Note the textual point: the Berean reads "Jesus" in Jude 5; other manuscripts read "the Lord".
2. **Election and choice; who is saved** (row 20, and noun-form rows 26 and 32). One list in the Bible's order with no side first: Isa 45:22, Matt 24:22, Mark 13:20, Luke 3:6, Luke 13:23, John 3:17, 4:42, 12:47, Rom 9:27, 11:26, 1 Cor 10:33, 1 Thess 5:9 ("appointed us ... to obtain salvation"), 2 Thess 2:13 ("God chose you from the beginning to be saved"), 1 Tim 2:4, 4:10, 2 Tim 2:10, Titus 2:11, 1 John 4:14. Calvinist/Reformed vs Arminian vs universalist readers: is one list the neutral form, or should it be two rows? It was two rows ("Few, a remnant, the chosen" / "Everyone, all, the world") and was joined for count. Rom 11:26 "all Israel" and Rom 9:27 "the remnant" are Paul's two statements about Israel; a Jewish reader should check that they sit as the text has them.
3. **Faith and works** (rows 13, 14, 15 verb; 26, 27 noun). One verse in every row it fits (Eph 2:8 in faith and grace; Jas 2:14 in faith and works; 2 Tim 1:9 and Titus 3:5 in works and grace). Row 14 has only 4 verses against 18 in row 13 because the key works verses (Eph 2:9-10) hold no word of "saved" and are named in the note only; the noun side (row 26, 14 verses) is larger. Does the 4 to 18 look like a tilt? Is the note's quoting of Eph 2:9-10 enough?
4. **Baptism** (row 16). Two verses (Mark 16:16, in the longer ending the earliest manuscripts lack; 1 Pet 3:21). Acts 2:40-41, Acts 16:30-33 and Titus 3:5 "the washing of new birth" are named in the note as not joined; the builder cannot join adjacent verses. Churches of Christ, Catholic, Orthodox, Lutheran, Baptist readers: is a two-verse row with that note enough, or does it hide the Acts narratives?
5. **"Asked and answered"** (row 19). One row holding the questions (Matt 19:25, Luke 13:23, Acts 16:30, Isa 64:5, 1 Pet 4:18) and what the text says is needed (Isa 45:22 "Turn to Me"; Isa 30:15 "repentance and rest"; Jer 4:14 "Wash the evil from your heart"; Acts 16:31 and Rom 10:9 "believe"; Rom 10:13 "calls"; 1 Cor 15:2 "hold firmly"; Jas 1:21 "accept the word"; Acts 15:1 "Unless you are circumcised ... you cannot be saved"). The note says Acts 15:11 answers Acts 15:1; a Jewish reader may hear the circumcision verse set as the wrong answer, and any reader may hear the list as a recipe. Should Acts 15:1 and its answer have a row of their own, or leave the list?
6. **The divine persons** (rows 1-6). Rows 1, 2, 3, 4 hold verses where the verse itself names the LORD or God, or is His own "I will save" (Isa 37:35, 2 Kgs 19:34, Jer 46:27, Zech 8:13, Isa 63:1, 63:5: the speaker is named in the verse before, not in the verse). Row 5 (Jesus) is by name or by Jesus' own "I" (John 5:34, 10:9, 12:47). Row 6 holds "the Lord", "Him", "the One who is able", "no other name": Rom 5:9-10 and Heb 7:25 say "through Him" with the name in the verse before. Is that the right place for Acts 4:12, and is 1 Tim 4:10 ("the Savior of everyone, and especially of those who believe", God by the context) rightly in the "Who is saved" row only?
7. **The cross** (row 9): "He saved others; He cannot save Himself" set as a row of its own. Christian readers may find it a hard label for the Gospels' words; the note says only whose words they are.
8. **Healing under "saved"** (row 12): the same Greek word σῴζω; the Berean's "healed", "made well", "restore". Does a reader take it as the page saying healing is salvation? The note says the page does not say which is meant.
9. **The Old Testament's salvation** (rows 1, 2, 7, 8, 30): most of the Hebrew uses are rescue from enemies in battle and from trouble (victory over the Philistines, the judges, "the LORD saved Israel that day"). A Jewish reader should confirm the order (Hebrew first, as the verb form leads with it) and that "A person saves" (judges, kings) is not read as a type of Christ. The Messianic readings (Zech 9:9, "righteous and victorious"; Jer 23:6) are left unmarked: Zech 9:9 sits under "Every other use" with a note that the page does not say whom it means.
10. **Rev 7:10** ("Salvation to our God, who sits on the throne, and to the Lamb!") is in the God rows by rule and in "Salvation is the LORD's": the note quotes it so no reader has to guess.
11. **Judg 10:13, Hab 1:2, Jer 14:9, Ps 22:1, Ps 3:2, Isa 59:11**: God not saving, in rows 8 and 31. Hard verses kept.

## What the builder could not express

- **Verbs and participles with no frame** (Hebrew passives and participles: 28 verses of this hub: 24 Hebrew, 4 Greek) are never "seen" in the verb form, so they join no rule row and no "Every other use". I found them by comparing the lexicon's occurrence list with the placed list by hand and picked every one. Option wanted: add every verse the verb word stands in to `seen_form`, so unplaced ones fall to "Every other use" or "More".
- **A pick in one form does not mark the verse placed in the other form.** Picking a noun-word verse (Ps 146:3, "who cannot save") into a verb row leaves it in the noun form's "Every other use" as well. I moved the noun-word verses into noun rows where a row fitted; four verses remain in both (2 Sam 10:11 and 1 Chr 19:12, Acts 4:12, 1 Tim 4:10). Option wanted: `placed` shared across forms, or a pick row spanning both.
- **A row condition on the English** (the Berean's "healed" against "saved"): the gloss rule reads MACULA, not the Berean, so the healing verses entered the faith row; I kept them out one by one with `not_verses` (9 verses). Option wanted: `english` regex condition on a row, read from the verse text.
- **Verses side by side** (Acts 2:40-41, Acts 16:30-33, Gal 3:26-27): no "this verse or the next" condition; named in notes only.
- **Verbless clauses** (Ps 3:8 "Salvation belongs to the LORD"): the noun rows read a word's neighbours inside one part of the clause, so subject and predicate in separate parts are not joined; those verses are by hand.
- **A per-example note** (1 Cor 3:15: "the Greek says 'work', the Berean 'it'"): the note is on the row, not under the verse.
- **Negation / polarity** ("I will no longer save you"; "no one to save") is not in the frames; those rows are by hand.
